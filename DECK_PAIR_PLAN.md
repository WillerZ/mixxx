## 1. Summary

A **deck pair** lets two decks (1+3, or 2+4) act as one deck with one transport while keeping
two completely independent audio **data planes** (each deck keeps its own `EngineBuffer`,
reader, scalers, stem decoding, and mixer channel). The deck that receives the `pair_enabled`
write is the **pair leader**: its track is the pair's track and its transport computation is
the pair's transport. The partner is the **pair follower**.

The shared thing is the **control plane**:

- **Transport authority** — a tiny lock-free object, `EnginePairTransport`, is the single
  instance of transport truth for the pair. Each engine callback the leader publishes a
  snapshot of its pair-plane progression (`posStart/posEnd/rate/speed/playing/scratching/
  reverse`); a *locked* follower consumes the snapshot and drives its own data plane from it
  instead of computing its own transport.
- **Command effect** — transport-affecting control writes on the follower are relayed (via a
  main-thread CO bridge) to the leader's existing engine machinery, and leader status COs are
  mirrored to the follower, so *existing* control interfaces (`play`, `hotcue_*`, `loop_*`,
  `beatjump*`, `rate`, `scratch2`, `jog`, …) keep working unchanged on both decks. The only
  new CO is `pair_enabled`.
- **Slip is the per-deck escape hatch — absolutely.** `slip_enabled`, `beatlooproll_*`, and
  `reverseroll` are **never bridged in either direction**: a roll *is* slip internally
  (`loopingcontrol.cpp:1343/1783`, `ratecontrol.cpp:308–316`), so anything that engages slip
  is deck-specific by construction. On the follower, Mixxx's existing slip plane
  (`m_slipPos`) is fed from the pair transport instead of advancing locally, so "own transport
  while slipping, snap back on release" falls out of the existing `processSlip()` code path
  with no new snap logic.

Terminology note: Mixxx already uses "leader/follower" for **BPM sync** (`SyncMode`,
`EngineSync`). This feature's roles are **pair leader/pair follower**; code uses the
`DeckPair*`/`PairRole` vocabulary to keep the concepts distinct. The mechanisms are
independent (a pair leader may be a sync follower; a pair follower's sync is neutralized
while paired).

Why leader/follower rather than symmetric "pair": the design is structurally asymmetric — one
engine computes transport per callback and the other replays it. Symmetric replication of two
independent transports was evaluated and rejected (§6.1) because it cannot guarantee the
frame-exact lock the vocal/instrumental use case demands.

### Primary DJ use case (drives the acceptance tests)

The instrumental plays on deck 1 and the a cappella on deck 3 (same track on both): pair from
one deck, **mute the vocal stem on one member and solo the vocal stem on the other** (stem
mute/solo are `[ChannelN_StemM]` groups and per-deck quick-effect properties — never shared).
Both channels then play the identical sample stream from their own readers, locked
sample-for-sample through hotcues, loops, seeking, jog/scratch, wheel and pitch. Slip on the
vocal deck lets the DJ scratch or roll the a cappella independently — the instrumental and the
pair transport keep playing — and disengaging slip snaps the vocal back onto it.

## 3. Codebase facts this design leans on (all verified in this tree)

1. **One engine callback thread processes all channels sequentially** —
   `EngineMixer::processChannels` (`enginemixer.cpp:234–360`) builds `m_activeChannels` and
   processes them in a `for` loop on the "Engine" thread; `EngineWorker`s exist for effects
   only. Existing **leader-first ordering precedent**: the BPM-sync leader channel is hoisted
   to index 0 (`enginemixer.cpp:246, 303–308`). §5.6 adds pair-leader-before-follower the same
   way. Without it the follower replays the *previous* callback's transport → a constant
   one-buffer (~5–23 ms) phase offset between vocal and instrumental — audible against a stem
   pair. With it, the lock is same-callback exact.
2. **CO notifications are synchronous on the writer thread;
  no engine - thread event dispatch exists in 2.7 * *(no `ControlEventQueue`/`broadcast()`/`processControlEventSignals()`).
   `ControlDoublePrivate::setInner` emits `valueChanged` inline(`control.cpp : 287–298`);
  `ControlObject` re - emits unless the setter is itself(self - echo suppression,
   `controlobject.cpp : 48–54`).Every engine CO connection is `Qt::DirectConnection` (`enginebuffer.cpp : 130, 136,…`)because the audio thread has no event loop.Consequences : engine slots are thin(atomics / COs only, mutation deferred to `process()`);
  a main - thread bridge writing COs reaches engine slots inline with** zero added latency**;
   `QueuedConnection` bridge proxies fed from the engine thread deliver on the main loop (fine
   for status mirrors; transport never waits on the bridge).
3. **`play` is the only confirm-required deck CO** (`enginebuffer.cpp:128–133`); the engine
   validates and `setAndConfirm`s (`slotControlPlayRequest`). Mirror writes to the follower's
   `play` flow through the follower's own validation — the gate confirms but skips transport
   side effects (§5.4.5).
4. **Transport truth is in EngineBuffer.** `processTrackLocked` order
   (`enginebuffer.cpp:901–1214`): cached geometry (`track_samplerate`, `track_samples`) →
   `processSyncRequests` → `play` flag + pitch/tempo ratio → `processSlip` → `processSeek`
   (the single quantization/phase-resolution funnel, `:1366–1441`) →
   `RateControl::calculateSpeed` → keylock/scaler policy (`:954–1014`) → scaler + advance →
   **`m_playPos` = `ReadAheadManager::getFilePlaypositionFromLog()`** (`:1141–1148`) →
   controls' `setFrameInfo`+`process` → `hintReader`; then `postProcess` (after ALL decks)
   publishes `playposition` (15 Hz), `VisualPlayPosition`, `beat_distance`. **Buffer-end
   position is not integrable from rate** — loop/jump triggers, trigger-overshoot correction
   and cache-miss padding relocate the read position mid-buffer
   (`readaheadmanager.cpp:58–64, 91–97, 141–152, 169–196`) — hence the shared snapshot
   publishes `(posStart, posEnd)` pairs, never a rate-only contract. All control-class seeks
   funnel through `EngineControl::seekAbs/seekExact` (`enginecontrol.cpp:85–95`) → `EngineBuffer`.
5. **Slip = "own transport until released", already implemented.** `slip_enabled` has **no
   slot** — polled once per callback at the top of `processSlip` (`enginebuffer.cpp:1284–1337`
   `+` edge detection vs `m_bSlipEnabledProcessing`), *before* `processSeek`/`calculateSpeed`
   in the same callback. Rising edge: `m_slipPos = m_playPos`; `m_dSlipRate = m_rate_old`
   **frozen** (later pitch/scratch/sync never touch it). Advance: at the frozen rate,
   loop/repeat-aware wrap via `adjustedPositionForCurrentLoopOrRepeat`
   (`loopingcontrol.cpp:1943–1968`), `SlipModeState::Armed/Running`. Falling edge:
   `seekExact(m_slipPos)` (applied same-callback by `processSeek`) unless
   `slipQuitAndAdopt()` (`:896–899`, single caller `LoopingControl::slotBeatLoopToggle`,
   `loopingcontrol.cpp:1759` — "adopt the rolled position"). `CueControl` has zero slip
   coupling. No `slip_started`/`slip_updated` COs exist; slip state is observable only via
   `slip_enabled` + `VisualPlayPosition`. Track load fully resets slip
   (`enginebuffer.cpp:584–589`).
6. **Rate/scratch funnel**: everything speed-related passes `RateControl::calculateSpeed`
   (`ratecontrol.cpp:400–516`) — `rateSearch` override, vinyl override, scratch2 via
   `PositionScratchController` (consumes `scratch2` accumulator; PD controller + spinback;
   loop-wrap compensation via `notifyWrapAround` from the readahead seam), `jog` (read **and
   zeroed**, `:376–394`, per-RateControl low-pass), `wheel` (ControlTTRotary), temp/perm
   buttons (`processTempRate` ramps — nudge equivalent), sync-PID (`calcSyncedRate`) last,
   reverse sign last. Skipping this one function per callback neutralizes a locked follower's
   transport inputs completely — and it *must* run exactly once per pair (it mutates state).
   Two-position scratch lives on `scratch_position`/`scratch_position_enable`
   (`positionscratchcontroller.cpp:86–115`). `whirl`/`nudge` do not exist in Mixxx — their
   equivalents (`wheel`/`jog`, `rate_temp_*`) are in the relay table.
7. **Loop/cue/hotcue state**: active loop lives in `LoopingControl::m_loopInfo` + `loop_*`
   COs; hotcue/cue/intro/outro **points live in the shared `Track` cue list** (persisted via
   `CueDAO`) — two decks of one `TrackPointer` already see identical points; only per-group
   *status* (`hotcue_N_status`, temp "loop" cue id `-1`) is per-deck. The follower's replica
   controls keep state coherent by reacting to mirrored CO writes.
8. **Same-track precedent**: the clone feature (`CloneFromDeck`, `m_pChannelToCloneFrom`
   `QAtomicPointer`, `SEEK_CLONE` `enginebuffer.cpp:1397–1404`, post-load rate/pitch/loop copy
   `basetrackplayer.cpp:707–730`) proves one `Track` on two decks and gives a **one-shot**
   load-time position copy — the pattern reused at pair formation (continuous coupling is the
   snapshot, §5.2, explicitly *not* repeated clone seeks).
9. **Main-thread load funnel**: `BaseTrackPlayerImpl::slotLoadTrack` (`final`) emits
   `loadingTrack(new, old)` (`basetrackplayer.cpp:571–574`) on **every** load attempt incl.
   clears; `playerEmpty` on eject completion (`:640`); `newTrackLoaded` on success (`:747`).
   Signal-level interception covers all entry points (library, QML, AutoDJ, next-available
   deck) where `PlayerManager::slotLoadTrackToPlayer` alone does not.
10. **Load/eject stall protocol**: `m_pause` is `tryLock`ed by `process()`
    (`enginebuffer.cpp:1244`); a deck whose load/eject is in flight emits silence, does **not**
    advance `m_playPos`, and `slotTrackLoaded` **drops a pending seek** (`:593`) and resets
    slip (`:584–589`). The lock must therefore re-anchor continuously (it does — every
    callback) and after load completion (§5.7, risk 9.2).
11. **Tests are single-threaded gtest**: fixtures never start the engine thread;
    `ProcessBuffer()` = `EngineMixer::process(1024)` synchronously; `MockScaler`
    (`MockedEngineBackendTest`) makes position math exact → frame-exact assertions are
    meaningful; `BaseSignalPathTest` builds 3 decks + `addDeck()` helper + real `sine-30.wav`
    (`SignalPathTest`) with golden-buffer audio asserts; CO pulse (`set(1); set(0);`) +
    `ProcessBuffer()` is the house idiom; `MixxxTest` deletes all COs after each test.

```text
 │      │   ├─ L→F status mirror (proxies)    │   │                                    │ snapshot   │
 │      │   └─ load/eject coordination        │   │  Follower Deck3: EngineBuffer       ▼ consume   │
 │      └─ (registers decks, roles)           │   │    (own reader/scaler/stems; gates: │ slip      │

 └────────────────────────────────────────────┘   └───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- `DeckPairManager` (new, `src/mixer/deckpairmanager.{h,cpp}`) — main-thread owner of pair
  lifecycle, validation, CO bridge, load/eject coordination.
- `EnginePairTransport` (new, `src/engine/deckpairtransport.{h,cpp}`) — the shared transport
  control plane: lock-free per-callback snapshot + role/active atomics. Two instances exist
  per session (one per pair slot); **never destroyed** (pointer handoff race-free by
  construction; only flags/contents change).

## 5. Design detail

### 5.1 The new CO: `[ChannelN], pair_enabled`

- Created by `DeckPairManager` for each deck group `[Channel1]`…`[Channel4]` as the decks are
  registered (decks appear via `PlayerManager::addDeckInner`, `playermanager.cpp:331–401`; add
  one call `m_pDeckPairManager->addDeck(i, pDeck)` there). The manager is constructed in the
  `PlayerManager` ctor next to `SamplerBank` (`playermanager.cpp:136`): constructed before any
  deck, and — being an earlier child — destroyed before them; its destructor must disarm all
  pairs first, so the engine pointers it handed to buffers are consult-only, never dangling.
- Write-validated `ControlObject`: manager connects `connectValueChangeRequest`
  (DirectConnection). Every write is arbitrated:
  - **1 (enable)** on X: refuse (leave 0) unless: X unpaired; partner exists
    (`numberOfDecks()` ≥ partner index); partner is not playing (R5). Accept →
    `setAndConfirm(1)`, form pair (**X = leader**), set partner's CO to 1 (guarded write),
    start engine roles + bridge, coordinate follower's track (§5.7).
  - **0 (disable)** on either member: `setAndConfirm(0)`, tear down (order below).
  - Redundant writes are no-ops.
- Not persisted (`bPersist=false`), no alias (no `[DeckN]` groups in this tree).
- **Teardown order** (unpair): 1) stop relay/mirrors; 2) clear pair-active atomic (engine stops
  publishing/consuming next callback); 3) release the follower slip-plane feed (a mid-slip
  follower becomes a normal slipping deck, snap target frozen at the last pair plane —
  documented, T31); 4) restore follower sync/vinyl acceptability. No position writes needed:
  the follower's `m_playPos` already *is* the pair plane.

### 5.2 `EnginePairTransport` — the shared transport control plane

```cpp
// src/engine/deckpairtransport.h
enum class PairRole : quint8 { Unpaired = 0, PairLeader = 1, PairFollower = 2 };

// Lock-free single-producer (leader buffer, engine thread) / single-consumer (follower
// buffer, engine thread, same callback, ordered after the leader). Publish/consume inside
// each buffer's m_pause window (same discipline as m_queuedSeek / ControlValueAtomic).
struct PairTransportSnapshot {
    double posStart, posEnd;   // pair-plane frame positions at leader buffer start/end
                               // (loop/trigger-wrap/seek aware — NOT rate-integrable)
    double rate, speed;        // applied sample-consumption rate; track-time/real-time speed
    bool playing, scratching, reverse;  // drive follower paused-flag + scaler policy (§5.4.1)
    quint32 seq;               // seqlock: odd = write in progress
};

class EnginePairTransport {             // one per pair slot, static lifetime
  public:
    void publish(const PairTransportSnapshot&);     // leader, engine thread
    bool consume(PairTransportSnapshot* out) const; // follower, engine thread
    QAtomicInteger<quint8> roleLeader, roleFollower; // set/cleared by DeckPairManager (main)
    QAtomicInteger<quint8> active;                  // pair live & transport shared
};
```

- **Pair plane** (the transport members lock to): leader slipping → the leader's slip plane
  `m_slipPos` (which keeps advancing during slip, with loop wraps); otherwise the leader's
  `m_playPos`. One expression, both cases. Consequences, all from existing slip semantics:
  leader slips → follower keeps playing the uninterrupted pair plane; leader slip-end rejoins
  it; a seek made on the leader during its slip moves the leader's *audible* plane only.
- Delivery into buffers: `EngineBuffer::setPairTransport(EnginePairTransport*, PairRole)`
  storing `QAtomicPointer<EnginePairTransport> m_pPairTransport` + `QAtomicInteger<quint8>
  m_pairRole` (handoff pattern precedented by clone's `m_pChannelToCloneFrom` `QAtomicPointer`,
  `enginebuffer.h:492`); surfaced through `EngineDeck` (`getEngineBuffer()` passthrough).
  Objects are never freed → no lifetime race; behavior gated by `active` + `role`.
- `isPairLockedFollower()` (engine-thread helper on `EngineBuffer`): `role == PairFollower &&
  active && m_slipModeState == Disabled && !slipEnabledNow` — reads the same
  `m_bSlipEnabledProcessing` the process path uses. **Locked mode** = consume the snapshot and
  suppress local transport effect.

### 5.3 Leader-side EngineBuffer changes (minimal diff, in `processTrackLocked`)

1. After the position-advance step, if `role == PairLeader && active`: publish
   `{posStart, posEnd, rate, speed, playing, scratching, reverse}` — the values the buffer
   just used/produced for its **main plane** (slip-plane values when the leader itself is
   slipping, per §5.2).
2. No other leader behavior changes. The leader's loops, cues, quantize, sync, vinyl, repeat
   remain its own engine machinery, unchanged. The pair transport *is* a fully-featured
   single-deck transport; only one copy of it runs.

### 5.4 Follower-side changes (the gates)

All gates read the §5.2 predicate once per callback (slot-side gates per invocation — they run
on the writer thread and touch atomics/COs only, the existing slot discipline):

1. **Position consumption** — in `processTrackLocked`, when locked: after `processSeek` and
   before scaling, `m_playPos := snapshot.posStart`; use snapshot `rate/speed` for scaler
   configuration instead of `RateControl::calculateSpeed` output (skipping the call alone
   neutralizes rate, temp/perm, wheel, **jog/scratch2 consumption**, reverse, sync-PID —
   fact §3.6), and adopt snapshot `scratching/reverse` for the keylock/scaler policy so both
   members choose the same scaler mode; after the scaler step set `m_playPos :=
   snapshot.posEnd` (authoritative overwrite over the read-log result → **drift structurally
   impossible**; frame-exact in MockScaler tests). `m_speed_old/m_rate_old/m_actual_speed`
   bookkeeping follows the snapshot so scaler re-selection behaves. The follower keeps its own
   `ReadAheadManager`/`CachingReader`/scalers, fed from the shared positions (data plane
   independent per R11).
2. **Seek suppression** — `queueNewPlaypos`/`seekAbs`/`seekExact` early-return when locked
   (before any crossfade-rescaler side effects — a re-seek mid-lock would click). All
   quantization/phase resolution for the pair happens once, on the leader's `processSeek`
   (fact §3.4); the follower's replica controls still react to mirrored COs (loop points,
   hotcue statuses) but their seeks are no-ops. The follower replica is display/slip-ready
   state, self-healing via L→F mirrors.
3. **Slip plane feed (the only slip interaction in the design)** — in `processSlip`, when
   `role == PairFollower && active && slipping`: instead of advancing `m_slipPos` at the
   frozen `m_dSlipRate` with local loop simulation, set `m_slipPos :=` leader pair plane
   (already loop- and seek-aware by construction). `m_slipModeState` is still set from the
   follower's own loop replica so waveform slip indicators stay coherent. Slip enable
   (rising-edge snapshot `m_slipPos = m_playPos` — already the pair plane while locked) and
   disable (`seekExact(m_slipPos)` → pair plane, same callback) need **no new code**: that is
   the snap-back. While slipping the follower is unlocked → its transport slots/seek/rate work
   locally with zero special cases (scratch, roll, hotcue-goto behave as on any slipping deck;
   the frozen slip rate guarantees scratch never perturbs the snap target).
4. **Publish nothing** — only the leader publishes; the follower can never move the pair plane
   twice.
5. **`slotControlPlayRequest` and friends** — when locked, still validate/confirm (so the
   mirrored `play` value lands) but skip transport effects and phase-sync requests
   (`requestSyncPhase`); the leader owns those. EOT is evaluated identically on both members
   (same pair position); the leader's `play=0` release at end-of-track is mirrored —
   idempotent (T21).
6. **`processSyncRequests`, BpmControl/KeyControl/ClockControl/QuantizeControl,
   `updateIndicators`, `hintReader`, `postProcess*`, `VisualPlayPosition`** — no changes:
   consistent automatically because the follower's position *is* the pair plane and its rate
   COs are mirrored leader values (`local_bpm`, `beat_distance`, `key`, `pitch`, `beat_active`
   recompute correctly per-deck — no mirror needed, keeping the mirror table small).
7. **Load interlock**: `slotTrackLoaded` resets slip and drops pending seeks (fact §3.10) —
   correct for pair loads; the lock re-anchors from the next consumed callback. `m_channelCount`
   may differ transiently while the follower loads — snapshot consumption guards on
   `isTrackLoaded()` and the follower emits silence (with play still mirrored) until loaded.

### 5.5 The CO bridge: relay (F→L) and mirror (L→F)

Owned by `DeckPair` (main thread), implemented with `ControlProxy`s parented to it (create/
destroy on main thread — required). Two static key tables + echo quiescence (needed because
trigger buttons use `ignoreNops=false` and always re-emit; value COs quiesce naturally via
`pSender` self-echo suppression + `ignoreNops=true` default — fact §3.2). Phase 2 implements
the quiescence as *equality*: a bridge write is skipped when its target already carries the
value — see deviation note 9.11 b).

- **Relay F→L** (only while bridged **and the follower is not slipping**; runs inline on the
  writing main thread ⇒ zero added latency to transport):
  `play, start_play, start_stop, stop, start, end, playposition, repeat, quantize, cue_*`
  (set/clear/goto/gotoandplay/gotoandstop/cue_default/cue_cdj/cue_play/play_stutter/
  cue_preview incl. press+release pairs), `hotcue_N_{
    activate, set, setcue, setloop, goto,
            gotoandplay, gotoandstop, gotoandloop, cueloop, activate*, clear, swap, color
}`,
  `loop_in, loop_out, loop_exit, loop_enabled, reloop_toggle, reloop_andstop, loop_remove,
  loop_anchor, loop_move*, loop_scale, loop_halve, loop_double, beatloop*, beatloop_size,
  beatjump*, beatjump_size*, rate, rate_range, rate_dir, rate_temp_*, rate_perm_*, reverse,
  fwd, back, wheel, scratch2_enable, scratch_position, scratch_position_enable,
  pitch_adjust, keylock, keylockMode, keyunlockMode, bpm (user-set path),
  beats_adjust_*/translate_*, bpm_tap, tempo_tap, sort_hotcues*, intro/outro set/clear`
  (accumulator steering for `jog`/`scratch2`, below).
  - **Accumulator steering** (`jog`, `scratch2` — consumed-and-zeroed by their owner,
    fact §3.6): relay slot does `L.co += F.co->get(); F.co->set(0)` (race-free: writers and
    relay are main-thread synchronous; the follower's consumer is skipped while locked).
    `scratch_position` relays as a plain value (not an accumulator).
  - The follower's own engine objects *also* react to their group's CO writes — intended:
    it maintains a live replica (loop points, hotcue statuses, rate state) that slip-engage
    can take over instantly and that skins/controllers display (§5.4.2).
- **Mirror L→F** (status/values, guarded): `play` (lands via F's confirm path, §5.4.5),
  `play_indicator, cue_indicator, play_latched, loop_enabled, loop_start_position,
  loop_end_position, beatloop_size, beatloop_%s_enabled, loop_anchor, cue_point,
  hotcue_N_status, hotcue_N_type, hotcue_N_position, hotcue_N_endposition, hotcue_N_color,
  intro/outro position+enabled+status, quantize, repeat, rate, rate_ratio, reverse, keylock,
  pitch_adjust, pitch, keylockMode, keyunlockMode, beatloop legacy mirror`.
  Deliberately **not mirrored** (recomputed coherently per §5.4.6): `playposition,
  beat_distance, local_bpm, bpm (engine-written side), key, pitch display,
  beat_next/prev/closest, beat_active, time elapsed/remaining, VisualPlayPosition`.
  Deliberately **never bridged, in either direction** (unit-testable invariant, T30): any
  slip key — `slip_enabled, beatlooproll_%s_activate, beatlooproll_r%s_activate,
  reverseroll` — plus all `[ChannelN_StemM]` COs, all mixer-strip COs, `vinylcontrol_*`,
  `sync_*`, `eject` (§5.7), `pair_enabled` (§5.1), `passthrough` (§5.8).
- **No slip coupling anywhere.** The follower's only slip-related code is the §5.4.3
  slip-plane feed (a position source while its own slip is engaged). There is no slip-state
  mirroring, arming window, or origin tracking — the bridge is a pure two-table lookup.
- **Ordering note**: manager proxies connect default `AutoConnection` to main-thread slots →
  engine-originated writes queue onto the main loop (display-rate statuses; transport never
  waits on the bridge); main-thread user writes relay synchronously inline.
  `ControlObject::set(key,…)` static (pSender=nullptr, bypasses self-echo suppression) is
  forbidden inside the bridge; all bridge writes go through the proxies.

### 5.6 Engine callback ordering (new: `EngineMixer` touch)

`processChannels` already hoists the sync-leader channel first (`enginemixer.cpp:246, 303–308`).
Add: while building `m_activeChannels`, hoist/insert each active pair's **leader before its
follower** (≤2 pairs; O(1) post-pass using `EngineBuffer::pairInfo()` atomics — e.g. move the
follower entry to immediately after the leader's). Query surface: `EngineMixer` asks each
channel's buffer for `activePairTransport()/role` (atomics; no buffer→buffer pointer
chasing). This guarantees the follower consumes a **same-callback** snapshot (§3.1: without
it, a permanent one-buffer stem offset; regression test T10 fails loudly).

### 5.7 Load / eject coordination (deck layer)

- **Pair formation**: if follower's loaded track ≠ leader's, follower loads the leader's
  track with `play = leader.play`, **preserving the follower's own stem selection** — reuse
  the clone path but *not* `cloneStemState` (`basetrackplayer.cpp:406–413`): a
  `slotCloneFromGroup` variant with a `StemChannelSelection` override parameter (or load +
  re-apply the follower stem mask). `SEEK_CLONE` (`m_pChannelToCloneFrom`, one-shot; note it
  reads the leader's `getExactPlayPos()` → VisualPlayPosition, which is exactly what we want
  at formation) aligns the first buffer; the lock maintains it afterwards.
- **Load on either member while paired** → load the same track on both: intercept
  `BaseTrackPlayer::loadingTrack(new, old)` (fires for *every* load entry point — fact §3.9),
  guarded against A→B→A bounce. Request on leader → follower `slotLoadTrack(new, bPlay=false)`
  (pair position authority continues; if leader is playing, follower loads with
  `bPlay=leader.play`). Request on follower → treated as a pair load: apply to the leader too
  (`pLeader->slotLoadTrack(new, …)`); the leader's transport restarts on the new track per
  normal load semantics.
- **Eject on either** → eject both: watch `playerEmpty`/`trackUnloaded`; guarded eject on the
  partner. Pair stays engaged (§2 R7 corollary).
- **Invariant**: `DEBUG_ASSERT` in the bridge (cheap `PlayerInfo` compare) that paired members
  hold the same track id (a divergence would mean a missed funnel).
- AutoDJ note: an AutoDJ handoff onto a paired deck loads both decks — correct and desirable;
  AutoDJ's own deck-selection logic is untouched.

### 5.8 Neutralizations & known shared-metadata effects

- **Sync on the follower**: `connectValueChangeRequest` bounce — refuse
  `sync_enabled/sync_mode/sync_leader` non-zero writes on the follower while locked (snap back
  to 0 + `qWarning`). Justification: a follower-side sync phase/tempo PID would
  double-correct against the pair lock (`calcSyncedRate`, `updateLeaderBpm` writing
  `rate_ratio`, `synccontrol.cpp:254–273`; `beatsync` phase seek), and an active follower
  Syncable would negotiate leadership for what is one transport (EngineSync leadership churn).
  Debug-assert `follower.syncMode == None` in the lock. Precedent: `SyncControl` already
  excludes follower+vinyl (`synccontrol.cpp:155–160`).
- **Vinyl control on the follower while locked**: refuse
  `vinylcontrol_wantenabled/vinylcontrol_enabled` (same bounce mechanism). Vinyl on the leader
  works normally (it *is* transport).
- **Passthrough / live input on either member**: refuse pairing while on; if toggled on a
  member mid-pair, disband the pair (`EngineDeck::process` short-circuits *before*
  `EngineBuffer::process`, `enginedeck.cpp:232–243` — the transport and the lock would
  freeze).
- **Shared `Track` cue list** (both decks share cue objects — fact §3.7): hotcue/intro/outro
  point edits on either deck affect both — consistent with "one deck". Known edges, accepted
  for v1 and documented: edits made while a member is slipping mutate the shared cues
  (last-writer-wins); the **temp loop cue (hotcue id −1)** is written by each deck's
  `LoopingControl::updateLoopCue` (`loopingcontrol.cpp:1912–1940`) — while locked the
  follower's loop points are mirrors. **Corrected in 9.18:** the "same-value
  writes" argument was wrong in both directions — the mirror writes are *not*
  same-value (the follower's own LoopInfo is written by the relayed gesture, and
  the two mirror writes do arrive as separate CO changes), and the feared
  remove/recreate of the shared temp loop cue does not happen; measured with and
  without a suppression gate the pair keeps exactly one loop cue with a stable
  `Cue` identity. No gate was added; T33 pins the invariant instead.
  reserved for Phase 5. Beatgrid edits (`beats_adjust_*`, `bpm_tap`) are shared-track metadata
  by nature — relayed once to the leader so a single write path applies them.
- **Slip-time hotcue SET on a slipping follower** stores the point at the follower's *audible*
  position into the shared Track (`frameInfo().currentPosition` semantics, existing) —
  documented edge, consistent with shared cue lists.

## 6. Alternatives considered (and why the chosen design is simpler/safer)

### 6.1 Bidirectional CO replication with two live transports (rejected)

Mirror every transport CO both ways and let both decks' full engine stacks run "as if".
Rejected: two state machines fed by eventually-equal command streams are **not** frame-exact
(quantize/loop decisions sample the position at write time; buffer-end position is not
integrable, fact §3.4) — for vocal+instrumental any drift is heard as flam/flange. Also
doubles every transport side effect (seek crossfades, sync PID, indicator state machines,
`calculateSpeed` mutation).

### 6.2 Share the data plane (one buffer feeding two channels) (rejected)

Explicitly out per requirements (R11); breaks per-deck stem gains/mutes and FX, which live
inside the data plane; makes unpairing a discontinuity.

### 6.3 One shared set of engine control objects owned by the leader; follower's destroyed

Cleanest "single brain", but destroys the follower's live replica (needed at slip-engage),
breaks the "every deck answers its own group's COs" invariant (skins/controllers read
`[Channel3]` COs nothing owns anymore), and makes unpair large engine-graph surgery. The
chosen design keeps both object graphs intact and symmetric; only gate flags + a snapshot.

### 6.4 Two EngineBuffers sharing one control group

Rejected (would fight over `play` confirm-required handlers, `playposition`
seek ping-pong, `jog`/`scratch2` consumption stealing, the per-group `VisualPlayPosition`
singleton — research items D–H). Each member keeps its own full group; the pair adds only
the out-of-band transport snapshot.

### 6.5 Symmetric "pair" terminology

The transport authority is structurally asymmetric (§1) — leader/follower names document the
data flow honestly. (Sync's leader/follower is BPM-only; new types are `DeckPair*`-prefixed.)

## 9. Risks, open items, mitigations

1. **Same-callback ordering coupling** (§5.6): a future channel-parallelizing refactor must
   re-establish leader-before-follower. T10 fails loudly on regression (one-buffer offset);
   `EnginePairTransport` could later gain staleness tolerance without interface change.
2. **Load/eject stall races** (fact §3.10): loading deck freezes position, drops pending
   seeks, resets slip. Mitigated by continuous re-anchoring (lock), one-shot `SEEK_CLONE` at
   formation, and explicit test T39.
3. **Dual `CachingReader`s of one Track**: 2× chunk-cache memory + 2× `hintAndMaybeWake`
   wakes on the shared `EngineWorkerScheduler`; a follower cache miss writes **silence but
   keeps position** (audio glitch, no desync). Phase-5 perf item (prefetch follows the pair
   plane because `hintReader` runs on consumed positions).
4. **`DirectConnection` discipline**: bridge slots write only COs/atomics and are
   reentrancy safe (T37). The relay refuses exactly one origin: writes issued from inside
   the audio callback on the callback thread (engine-internal status), via
   `EngineMixer::isEngineCallbackThread()`; commands from every other thread — GUI *and
   the controller engine thread, which is how hardware delivers input* — relay inline,

 a) **Status keys that the engine rewrites as a *side effect* of a relayed command must not
    be relayed** (supersedes §5.5 listing `loop_enabled`, `beatloop_size`, `beatjump_size`

 b) **Mid-roll loop adoption needs its own quiescence window** (found by T30):
    `slipQuitAndAdopt()` clears the follower's `slip_enabled` *synchronously inside* the
    command handler that the follower's own engine runs before the bridge listener, so the
    "slip disconnects the bridge" guard (9.11 m) sees the slip already gone and would relay

 c) **Preview machinery must not run on a locked follower** (found by T18): the follower's
    own `hotcueActivatePreview` writes `play` inline on the writer thread, *ahead of* the
    relayed button press, so the leader takes its already-playing branch (plain goto, no
    preview/return state) and the return-on-release is lost; the same happens on release in
    reverse. `CueControl` now skips preview handling while `isPairLockedFollower()` — the
    command reaches the leader via the bridge and the follower rides the pair plane; its
    preview status arrives on the mirror. (Cue-preview CO `cue_preview` gated likewise;

    idempotent through the play mirror, T23 pins the observable behaviour.) Note this
    tree's single-deck preview-release semantics: the deck stops *at the previewed cue
    point* (the cached return position is the cue position, not the pre-preview position) —
    the pair reproduces exactly that.

 d) **Steered accumulator set is {`jog`} only, not {`jog`, `scratch2`}** (found by
    T16; final shape by note i): the plan (fact §3.6) assumed `scratch2` is
    consumed-and-zeroed by its owner; RateControl only *reads* it — the scratch source
    (mapping/vinyl) owns the value contract, and nothing zeroes it. Copy-relay (leader
    mirrors the follower's value) is therefore the correct semantic for `scratch2`;
    steering it (add + zero) would integrate absolute velocity writes into a runaway

    on both members. Only `jog` — whose single consumer reads-and-zeroes it — needs any
    special transfer at all, and it turns out that transfer cannot live in the CO bridge
    (note i and §9.13): it rides the pair transport's accumulator lane.

 e) **The shared-Track beatgrid family (`bpm`, `beats_adjust_*`, `beats_translate_*`) is

    the acting deck's BpmControl applies it once; both engines adopt the new grid via the

 g) **`pitch`/`key` are not mirrored** (§5.4.6 over §5.5's "pitch" entry): the follower

 h) **A locked follower still runs the skipped accumulator consumer** (bench round 1;

### 9.13 Bench round 3 (SC Live 4) — the accumulator lane and the loop replica

Implemented in `EnginePairTransport` (accumulator lane), `RateControl`/`EngineBuffer`
(consume-and-publish, claim-on-consume), `LoopingControl` (roll gate) and the
`DeckPairManager` re-anchor listener. Written autonomously by the AI assistant as the
follow-up to the round-2 bench; the human developer must re-verify on the rig.

**`jog` crosses on the pair transport's accumulator lane, not the CO bridge**
(a resolution of note i): the locked follower's per-callback consume step
(`EngineBuffer` → `RateControl::pairFollowerConsumeAccumulators()`, note h) now
*publishes* what its read-and-zero discarded onto a latest-wins slot in
`EnginePairTransport`; the pair leader's `RateControl::getJogFactor()` **claims** the
lane into its own consumption, exactly where a single deck would have consumed the
same value. Both contracts then behave single-deck-exactly for free: overwrite
happens on the follower's CO before its consume (latest velocity), accumulation
happens there too (`+=` bursts sum), and each value reaches the leader's rate
precisely once. One publish per follower callback makes the slot latest-wins (a
stalled leader — loading — cannot accumulate a stale lump). Latency: one callback,
which is the delay a post-consumption write has on a single deck anyway. `jog` is
removed from the relay table; the table-invariant test pins its absence from both
tables because every CO transfer of it failed the bench while passing write-once
tests. NaNs are dropped at publish (poisoned wheel positions) as `getJogFactor`
drops them; `reset()` silences the lane with the pair. Regression:
`DeckPairEngineTest.JogAccumulatorCrossesThePairLaneExactlyOnce` (negative control:
publishing a constant or a sum, or skipping the claim, fails it outright) and the
reworked T16, which now measures the *steady-state* rate effect per buffer (the old
window-sum thresholds were effect-zero tolerant — the very test weakness the bench
kept discovering).

**A deck-local roll must not consume the pair loop's replica** (bench round 3, found by
T40): the SC Live mapping's roll pads press `beatlooproll_activate`, whose first-press
semantics on a deck with a running loop are "exit the loop first, roll on the next press".
On a locked follower the running loop is the leader's mirrored replica, so the first press
(a) never engaged the roll and (b) destroyed the replica — and since the mirror is
edge-triggered, the leader's unchanged `loop_enabled` never re-pushed it. That is the
DJ's whole round-3 loop report: rolls with wrong/absent lengths and a later manual loop
existing "only on the lead deck" (the follower's loop machinery was no longer following).

`DeckPairEngineTest.FollowerRollEngagesOverThePairsLoopOnFirstPress` with negative
controls: `LoopingControl::slotBeatLoopRollActivate` lets a locked pair follower skip the
loop-exit branch (the roll stores the replica and restores it around the gesture, like an
unpaired deck — R8 stays intact: the roll is deck-local, the loop stays the pair's); and
the slip-release re-anchor gained an idempotent trailing pass from the event loop, because
a slip release can be one write inside a longer command transaction (roll release =
loop-disable + slip-release + loop-restore) whose remaining writes would otherwise land
after an inline re-anchor. `DeckPairEngineTest.LeaderLoopAfterFollowerRollCycleReachesReplica`
pins the DJ's exact sequence (roll pads, then a plain leader loop).

*Phase-4 carry-overs unchanged:* re-sync the follower's internal rate state before

### 9.14 Bench round 4 (SC Live 4) — rolls on the pair leader, and the queued roll press

Implemented in `LoopingControl` (pair-member roll gate, leader shared-loop
re-establishment, synchronous `beatlooproll_activate` delivery) and
`EngineBuffer` (`isPairLeaderActive()`). Written autonomously by the AI assistant as the
follow-up to the round-4 bench; the human developer must re-verify on the rig.

The DJ's round-4 report after the round-3 fix: intermittently *"pad pressed and nothing
happens"*, and *"pad pressed and both decks of the pair are looped"* — most presses behave,
so timing was suspected. It is timing, and the mechanism is the **leader-side twin** of
§9.13, amplified by the roll button's delivery thread:

- The mapping's roll-pad press writes `beatloop_size` and then `beatlooproll_activate`
  (`Denon-SC-Live-4-scripts.js:2474-2490`). The size write runs inline on the writing
  controller thread (change-request path, `Qt::DirectConnection`) and **resizes a running
  loop in place, keeping it enabled**; `beatlooproll_activate` was one of the two loop
  buttons left on the default (Auto → *queued to the main thread*) delivery, so on
  hardware the press landed a main-loop latency after the resize.
- Press on the pair **leader** while the pair loops: the pair transport's live loop becomes
  the roll length, still enabled — any engine callback landing in the gap plays the wrapped
  loop, publishes it as the pair plane, and the mirror re-points the replica's region:
  **both decks audibly loop at the pad length** until the queued press lands ("both decks
  in the pair are looped"). Whether a callback lands in the window is buffer timing, which
  is why it is intermittent and invisible to the single-threaded test engine (9.11 h).
- When the queued press finally ran, the leader still had the vanilla semantics: the §9.13
  gate exempted the locked follower *only*, so the leader took the "exit the running loop"
  first-press branch — **no roll, and the pair loop destroyed** ("nothing happens"). Even
  with the roll engaged, the vanilla release would have ended the shared loop on both
  decks (restore-without-enable).

Fix (deck-pair semantics in the engine, no controller binding change — R8: the roll is
deck-local, the loop stays the pair's):

- The `slotBeatLoopRollActivate` gate now accepts **either member of an active pair**
  (`EngineBuffer::isPairLeaderActive()`, new, next to `isPairLockedFollower()`): a leader
  roll press rolls over the shared loop instead of consuming it. The roll is deck-local
  through slip — while the leader slips, the pair plane is the leader's slip plane (§5.2),
  so the follower plays uninterrupted.
- On the last deck-local roll release the leader **re-enables the restored loop** when the
  stored loop had been enabled (new `m_bPrevLoopWasEnabled` in `storeLoopInfo()`): the
  vanilla restore leaves it disabled, which for the leader means the pair stops looping on
  both decks the moment the pad is released. This is the leader-side counterpart of the
  follower's slip-release mirror re-anchor (9.11 m / 9.13). The same re-establishment was
  added to the size-coded family's last-roll release (`slotBeatLoopDeactivateRoll`) so the
  rule holds for both roll CO families.
- `beatlooproll_activate` is delivered via `Qt::DirectConnection` now, like every other
  loop-family button in that constructor (the `BeatLoopingControl` roll buttons were always
  direct): the mapping's size-write→roll-write transaction becomes atomic against the
  engine callback, closing the both-looped window on hardware. This restores for the
  generic roll CO the same behavior the size-coded buttons and the unpaired bench testing
  already had; the mapping is untouched.

Regression: `DeckPairEngineTest.LeaderRollLeavesThePairLoopRunningAndRollsOnFirstPress`
(T42) pins the mapping's full dance on the leader — press (size, roll), hold with the pair
plane advancing under the leader's slip, release, size restore — asserting slip on the
first press, the pair loop alive on both members throughout, and the pair region restored
and mirrored. Negative controls on both halves: with the gate off the press takes the
loop-exit branch (slip assertion fails); with the re-enable off, `loop_enabled` drops to 0
on both members at release. T40/T41 (follower side) unchanged and green; the leader's roll
interleaving with the bridge needed no manager changes (the roll family is in neither
bridge table, and the loop_enabled 1→0→1 blip at the leader's release rides the normal
mirror edge path).

### 9.15 Bench round 5 (SC Live 4) — the threading-model integration harness, and what it found

Test infrastructure (`DeckPairThreadedTest`, DECK_PAIR_TEST T43); implementation code is
**unchanged** this round after a hardening experiment was implemented and then backed out
(see below). Written autonomously by the AI assistant as the follow-up to the round-5
bench; the human developer must re-verify on the rig.

The DJ's round-5 report: follower-initiated rolls clean; leader-initiated rolls and
slicer pads "affecting both decks, including enabling slip on the follower deck". No
static path exists that could do this — every writer of a `slip_enabled` control is that
deck's own `EngineBuffer` / `LoopingControl`, or the mapping writing its own group — and
the single-threaded suites cannot see cross-thread interleavings at all (9.11 h). The
round-5 ask was therefore to *build the missing instrument first*: an automated
integration test with a realistic threading model.

T43 runs the three production threads against the real engine: an **engine thread**
calling `EngineMixer::process()` continuously (a callback can interleave any two writes
of a mapping transaction), a **controller thread** replaying the SC Live roll/slicer CO
sequences verbatim with microsecond jitter between writes (direct-connected loop slots
execute inline on it, as on hardware), and the **main thread** pumping the Qt event loop
throughout (queued mirror deliveries, the slip-release re-anchor, its trailing
`QTimer::singleShot(0)` pass). The scenario runs both members' roll and slicer pads —
sequentially and **overlapping mid-hold** (two fingers), including the pad-switch
sequence and follower-side loop exit/re-entry through the relay — and asserts: R8 (no
deck's `slip_enabled` may rise outside its own gesture, with the *writing thread*
attributed in the violation log), no stuck slip, and loop-state convergence at each
cycle boundary. Four findings:

1. **Observation must happen at the ControlProxy layer.** `ControlObject::valueChanged`
   is suppressed when the registered control instance itself is the writer
   (`privateValueChanged` drops `pSender == this`), and the engine-internal writers
   (roll engage/release via member pointers, pending activation) write exactly that way.
   A first spy generation attached at the CO signal and was blind for precisely the
   transitions under investigation; `DeckPairManager` listens through proxies (which see
   every change whatever the sender), so T43 spies do too. Any future pair instrumentation
   must follow the same rule.
2. **The harness caught its own instance of the bench's error class.** The first versions
   reported reproducible "loop_enabled diverged" failures — they were produced by the
   harness itself: its main thread stopped pumping events while `join()`-ing the
   controller thread, freezing the main loop's half of the system (mirror deliveries,
   re-anchor, timers all stop) for the rest of the scenario. A GUI thread never blocks on
   its input thread; the main thread now pumps until the scenario retires, and the
   phantom failures disappeared. This is the same "half the system stood still" class the
   round-4 queued-press bug created inside the engine — worth remembering in both
   directions.
3. **The detector is proven, and the reported leak is not present in this tree.**
   Injecting a synthetic R8 leak (leader's roll press also setting the partner deck's
   `slip_enabled`) is caught at the first press of cycle 0, with the writing thread named
   in the violation. With the injection removed, repeated full soaks show no follower
   slip from leader gestures, no loop divergence, and exact final convergence — for
   roll, slicer, pad-switch, overlap and relay paths alike. Combined with the static
   writer audit (no code path writes a `slip_enabled` across decks), the round-5 symptom
   is not reproducible against the current tree. *Bench item:* rebuild the application
   binary from this exact working tree (a round-4 build mixed with round-3 object files
   would explain the symptom; the 10:47 `loopingcontrol.cpp` and 10:36 `enginebuffer.cpp`
   changes must both be in the tested binary) and re-run the reported pad dance.
4. **A convergence sweep was implemented, measured, and backed out.** A 20 ms
   level-triggered re-anchor of every pair's replica (`refreshMirrorValues`, gated while
   *either* member slips) briefly lived in `DeckPairManager`. Negative control: T43
   passes identically without it — its motivating "stale queued emission equality-skipped
   the delivery" pattern turned out to be finding 2 in disguise, and the edge-triggered
   mirror plus slip-release re-anchor (9.11 l/m) proved watery under the faithful
   threading model. The sweep is out; the engine side of this branch is exactly the
   round-4 state. If a future bench round shows genuine replica drift, the sweep design
   (interval timer on the manager, skip while either member slips, idempotent refresh)
   is the shape that was validated as safe.

### 9.16 Bench round 6 (SC Live 4) — rolls on the leader rolled *both* decks: the missing leader-side mirror halt

The DJ rebuilt from this working tree and re-ran the pads with stems split across the pair
(vocal audible on the leader, instrumental on the follower), which makes the pair's two
planes audible at once. Result: **every** roll pad press on the leader rolled *both*
decks — the instrumental followed the vocal into the roll region and back — while rolls on
the follower stayed deck-local, and the round-5 "slip stuck on the follower" symptom was
confirmed gone. Deterministic, leader-only, loop-shaped: the report itself named the bug.

**Root cause:** the L→F mirror halt for slip (9.11 m) gated only on the *follower's*
`slip_enabled`. While the leader rolls, its gesture statuses stream over the mirror like
any other status: the roll's `loop_start/end_position` + `loop_enabled` (the follower
engages the roll region as a real loop), the roll size's forced `beatloop_*_enabled`, and
the per-callback `playposition` — which during a slip is the leader's *audible,
wrap-inside-the-roll* position, re-seeking the follower into the roll region at main-loop
rate. The transport lane itself was always correct (the pair plane rides the leader's slip
plane, 5.2, loop-unaware while `isLoopRollActive()`); only the status mirror leaked the
gesture. Rounds 3–5 could not see it: before the round-4 leader gate the roll often did
not engage at all (vanilla loop-exit first-press), and every existing test — T40–T42
included — samples loop state only at cycle boundaries, where the release re-anchor
(9.11 m/9.13) had already healed the replica. Making the roll engage reliably made the
leak reliable.

**Fix (symmetry with 9.11 m):** `DeckPairManager::slotBridgeMirror` halts while the
*leader* slips too, including its `slipQuitAndAdopt` pending window (T30 counterpart); a
leader-side `slip_enabled` listener re-anchors the replica on release with the same guards
and trailing pass as the follower's; `refreshMirrorValues()` itself now skips while **any**
member slips, so the last release in the pair performs the anchor (this also closes the
double-roll hole where a follower slip release pushed the rolling leader's statuses onto
its own live roll). Cosmetic consequence, mirroring the accepted 9.11 m behavior: the
follower's display-only mirrors (playposition, loop indicators) freeze for the duration of
a leader roll; the audio plane never stops (it rides the transport lane). *Bench item:*
the leader's waveform on the follower's screen during a leader roll. Pinned by T44
(mid-gesture unit assertions, red before the fix) and the T43 follower-loop-region spy
(mirror deliveries attributed to the main thread are violations while the leader slips).

### 9.17 Phase 4 implementation — the slip-plane feed, the re-lock re-anchor, and what the matrix pinned

Implemented in `EngineBuffer::processSlip()` / `processTrackLocked()` plus the new test file
`src/test/deckpairslip_test.cpp` (T13 ×2, T15a, T25, T27–T29, T31; 50/50 deck-pair tests and
the full unit suite green). Written autonomously by the AI assistant; the human developer
must re-verify every gesture on the rig before anything goes upstream.

**The feed (§5.4.3) is a position copy, not a rate.** While `role == PairFollower` and the
pair is active, a slipping follower's `m_slipPos` is set to the *consumed snapshot's
`posEnd`* every callback instead of advancing at the frozen `m_dSlipRate` with the vanilla
loop simulation. Because the leader publishes in the same callback (EngineMixer ordering,
5.6), the follower's snap-back target equals the pair plane with *exact* double equality —
every test asserts `EXPECT_DOUBLE_EQ`, and T13/T25/T27/T28/T29 all hold it. The pair plane's
loop/seek awareness (T29 lands the release inside the pair loop after a wrap) is inherited
from the leader's own machinery, exactly as the design predicted — no follower-side loop
code needed. `m_slipModeState` is picked from the follower's own loop replica exactly as the
vanilla branch picks it (T29 pins `Armed` while the mirrored pair loop runs under the slip).
`consume()` failure (fresh pair, leader loading) falls through to the vanilla local advance,
so a follower never snaps to an invented position; once the leader publishes, the feed
re-anchors the plane.

**Gap found while implementing, fixed: the re-lock re-anchor.** On the slip-release edge the
vanilla snap-back `seekExact(m_slipPos)` is suppressed by the 5.4.2 seek gate — by that
point the follower is locked again (the CO is already 0), and the lock itself performs the
position snap. That left the *data plane* (reader + scaler) on the audible plane whenever
the pair plane had not moved in the meantime: with a paused pair, the DJ scrubs or rolls
during the slip, releases, and the continuity check (`pairPosStart == last posEnd`) sees no
discontinuity while the reader still points at the scrubbed position. The release edge now
sets `m_pairReanchorOnRelock` when paired; the first consumed snapshot after the re-lock
performs the same silent `notifySeek` + `m_pScale->clear()` re-anchor the leader-jump path
uses (5.4.1) — no click, and it also covers the slip-quit-and-adopt variant of the hole.
Reset with the rest of the pair state at disarming.

**9.11 d carry-over resolved by verification (T25/T31 pin it).** The follower's rate book-
keeping *is* synchronised at slip-engage: while locked, the scaler-parameter path runs with
the snapshot speed, so `m_rate_old` tracks the pair rate and the rising edge's frozen
`m_dSlipRate` equals the leader's current `m_rate_old` (asserted frame-exact in T25/T31).
Rate-control internals need no re-sync: the rate COs are mirror-fresh until the slip (and
the release re-anchor re-pushes whatever the pair changed mid-slip — T25 asserts the
follower's `rate`/`rate_ratio` carry the leader's mid-slip 1.25 after release); the temp
ramp restarts from the fresh button COs like on any live deck; and `PositionScratchController`
re-initialises its reference positions on scratch enable, so a scratching slipping follower
(T27) cannot inherit a stale scratch state. What *is* deck-local by construction — the
audible rate during the slip — is exactly what R8 wants.

**T31 semantics, as shipped:** unpairing a slipping follower releases only the feed. Slip
stays engaged, play state stays, the leader is untouched; the snap target then advances
*locally at the rate frozen at slip-engage* (plain-deck behaviour) rather than being
literally frozen at the last pair plane — the plan's §5.1 wording "frozen at the last pair
plane" is documented here as *starting from* the last pair plane and advancing normally,
which is what "becomes a normal slipping deck" means operationally (T31 pins both halves,
including that the eventual release snaps to the follower's own plane while the ex-partner
is already away).

**T15a mechanism (pinned, no code change):** `RateControl::slotReverseRollActivate` sets
`slip_enabled` *before* `reverse` on press and clears `reverse` *before* `slip_enabled` on
release, so every `reverse` write lands inside the slip window where the relay refuses —
the pair never sees the reverse, and the follower's reverse survives because the mirror is
halted for the whole slip (9.11 m/9.16).

**Test ergonomics:** a `TEST_F` body derives from the fixture, so it cannot use the
`friend class DeckPairSlip{,AudioTest}` grant directly — EngineBuffer internals are read
through small fixture accessors (`slipPos()`, `slipRate()`, `rateOld()`,
`slipModeState()`), the fixture being the friend. Wrap-based tests (T13, T29) run on the
real-scaler `DeckPairSlipAudioTest` fixture; the mocked fixture never wraps (9.12 note).

### 9.18 Phase 5 implementation — hardening, three audits, two changes refused, and the bench protocol

Implemented in `deckpairmanager.{h,cpp}`, `enginebuffer.{h,cpp}`,
`autodjprocessor.cpp`, `controlpickermenu.cpp`, plus `deckpair_test.cpp`, the new
`deckpairstem_test.cpp` and `autodjprocessor_test.cpp`. Deck-pair suite 90 tests
green (`DeckPair*`, `EnginePairTransport*`, `AutoDJ*`). Written autonomously by
the AI assistant; **the human bench sign-off required by `AGENTS.md` has not
happened** — see `DECK_PAIR_BENCH_PROTOCOL.md`, which is the deliverable for that
gate and whose results table is deliberately empty.

**What changed in production code, and why each one is justified by evidence
rather than by suspicion.** Phase 5 ran three read-only audits (dual-reader
cost, shared metadata, passthrough/AutoDJ interlocks) and each produced a
prediction. Two predictions were measured, found not to hold, and the
corresponding change was *refused*; three were confirmed and fixed. Recording
the refusals matters as much as the fixes — they are the parts a reviewer would
otherwise re-litigate.

*Refused 1 — temp loop cue suppression (the shared-metadata audit's main
finding, and the one this phase was scoped to decide).* The audit predicted the
locked follower would corrupt the shared `Track`'s temp loop cue: both members
hold the same `Track`; `LoopingControl::updateLoopCue()` is a check-then-act on
the shared cue list; the follower is fed the leader's region as two separate
mirror writes while its own `LoopInfo` is supposedly stale, so the first write
looks torn and calls `removeTempLoopCue()` on the leader's cue and the second
recreates it as a *new* `Cue` — a lost loop cue for a main-loop iteration, a new
`DbId`, colour and label gone, three shared-`Track` mutations where an unpaired
deck makes one. An `isPairLockedFollower()` guard was implemented accordingly
(the same gate `RateControl`/`CueControl` use). It was then removed again:
measured both ways, with the gesture issued on *either* member, the pair produces
exactly one loop cue with a **stable cue identity** and a single `cuesUpdated`
burst per gesture. The premise is wrong — the relayed gesture also runs on the
follower, so its `LoopInfo` is in step and no write is ever torn. Suppressing a
whole writer on the shared `Track` without a demonstrated fault trades a
hypothetical bug for a real behaviour change. The regression test stays
(T33 `LoopCueOnTheSharedTrackIsWrittenOnlyTheLeader`) with `loopingcontrol.cpp`
carrying a comment that says why there is no guard. **Consequence for 5.8: the
"the follower must not write shared metadata" argument that 5.8 gave for the
temp loop cue is not needed and must not be quoted as a reason for a gate.**

*Refused 2 — a new engine hook for per-deck audio.* §9.3 asked whether the
dual-reader design needs an engine-side tap to compare member audio. It does
not: `EngineMixer::getChannelBuffer(group)` already exposes per-deck output and
T32 uses it. No engine change.

*Fixed 1 — a refused propagated load now disbands the pair (5.7, Gap A).*
`propagateTrack()` with `LoadWhenDeckPlaying::Reject` used to leave the pair
engaged across two different tracks. That state is not a degraded pair: a locked
follower replays the leader's *frame positions* against whichever track it
holds, i.e. two unrelated tracks at one transport, with nothing to detect or
heal it. The refusal path now disbands. Two tests (`PropagatedLoad…`,
`EjectHonours…`) had pinned the old divergence-as-acceptable behaviour and were
re-pinned to the disband; that they passed before is the usual lesson that a
test asserts what it was written against, not what is true. A
`checkSameTrackInvariant()` DEBUG_ASSERT was added to both bridge slots to make
the divergence audible in a debug build if it is ever reintroduced.

*Fixed 2 — formation no longer silently skips track adoption.* Found while
re-pairing in the Gap A tests: `synchronizeTracks()` adopted via
`cloneFromChannel()`, which takes the track from the source deck's **engine
buffer** and returns silently when a load is still in flight there. Unpair and
re-pair promptly — a normal DJ sequence — and formation "succeeded" with the
members on two different tracks. Formation now falls back to a plain
`slotLoadTrack()` of the leader's deck-level track when the clone source is not
ready; the clone only contributes frame alignment of the first buffer, which
needs a loaded source anyway and is irrelevant while the pair is not yet
running.

*Fixed 3 — thread affinity of the `pair_enabled` and `passthrough` listeners
(Gap C).* Both were `Qt::DirectConnection`, so a mapping on a controller thread
writing either control ran pair formation or teardown — creating and destroying
the members' `ControlProxy` objects — on the *controller* thread. Both are now
`Qt::AutoConnection`: a same-thread write (skin button) stays synchronous, a
foreign writer is deferred onto the manager's thread, matching the leader-side
mirror listener in `buildBridge()`. `PassthroughFromAnotherThreadDisbandsSafely`
writes from a `std::thread`, waits for the write *without* running the main
event loop, and asserts the pair is still intact; it fails with
`DirectConnection` and passes now. Passthrough was also extended to the leader
side in the existing test — the leader is the member where a stuck passthrough
mutes the transport the partner is replaying.

*Fixed 4 — AutoDJ is no longer locked out by its own mirror (Gap D).* Decks 1+2
bound for the crossfader, 1+3 paired, deck 1 playing: the partner reads as
playing and `toggleAutoDJ()` returned `ADJ_UNUSED_DECK_PLAYING` — an error the
DJ cannot act on, because stopping the partner stops the pair.
`isPairPartnerOf()` skips a candidate that is the partner of a bound deck
*while the pair is engaged*; an unpaired third deck playing still refuses, which
`Decks34PlayingWarning` and the negative half of the new test both pin. The
check reads the `pair_enabled` controls rather than reaching for
`DeckPairManager`: the library layer has no handle on the manager, and acquiring
one for this would be the larger coupling.

*Fixed 5 — the follower now detects a stalled pair plane (Gap E).*
`EnginePairTransport::consume()` is a seqlock read: it hands out the last
published value forever, and says nothing about freshness. A leader that stops
publishing while the follower stays locked was therefore not a stall but a
*discontinuity on every single callback* (`posStart` never equals the previous
`posEnd` once the pair was ever moving), so the follower called
`notifySeek()` and cleared its scaler every callback — thrashing its reader and
its chunk cache indefinitely, while adopting a stale position and a stale speed.
`EngineBuffer` now remembers the last consumed snapshot and treats an unchanged
one as no snapshot: hold position, speed and rate 0. A paused leader publishes an
unchanged snapshot deliberately, and holding is also the right answer for it, so
the two cases need no distinction. State is reset in `setPairTransport()`, i.e.
on formation and teardown. The comment at the speed selection that claimed the
no-snapshot case was "e.g. while the leader is loading" was factually wrong
(`consume()` returns the stale value there, not `false`) and now says what
actually happens.

**Tests added.** T26 (`deckpairstem_test.cpp`, STEM-gated, 2/2) is the stem-split
statement of the architecture: identical settings ⇒ identical audio, split stems
⇒ one transport and two data planes, with silence-aware sampling because both
members can miss the same chunk. T32 (real scalers) is the audio-level statement
of the lock: bit-exact member buffers in steady state, across a rate change and a
loop. Both were written *before* their flakiness was understood, and both taught
the same lesson twice, so it is recorded here: **strict per-callback sample
equality is not testable against a real reader.** A `CachingReader` miss emits
silence and then ramps back in to avoid a pop (9.3), and the length of that
window depends on how fast the reader thread happens to decode. Demanding
equality through it produced a test that failed one run in five. T32 now asserts
*position* strictly on every callback — the pair plane is exact by construction —
and watches audio for **sustained** disagreement, which is what a drifting
follower actually looks like; the isolated-burst tolerance is named, counted and
logged rather than silent. T34 is the long soak (2000 callbacks plus a
pause/resume cycle that also exercises Gap E's recovery path); T36 covers
re-pairing and role swapping, and pins that re-pairing onto an audible partner is
**refused** (R5) — so after F1-style refusal the DJ must stop a deck first, which
is a design decision the bench protocol asks the developer to reconfirm rather
than silently accept (bench step F4).

**`co_dump` and the picker.** `[Channel1..4],pair_enabled` is present in
`co_dump_inital.csv`; there is no automated consumer of that file in-tree, so
"regenerate the dump" reduces to it being there, which it is. The
`ControlPickerMenu` entry (`Transport -> Deck Pair`) is what makes the control
mappable, and its description text is the DJ-facing documentation: it names slip,
loop rolls and reverse roll as deck-specific, because a DJ who maps the button
learns that from the picker rather than from this file.

**Manual and mapping documentation.** The Mixxx manual lives in a separate
repository, so the user-facing wording is provided here ready to paste rather
than edited in place, and per `AGENTS.md` a human must submit it:

> When two decks are paired, they play one track as one deck: transport, tempo,
> loops and cues act on the pair, whichever deck you touch. Three things stay
> with the individual deck on purpose: slip mode, and any gesture that holds the
> track in place — a hotcue roll, a beatloop roll, reverse roll, or a scratch.
> The other deck keeps playing while you do them. Each deck also keeps its own
> mixer strip and its own stem mute/solo/gain, so a pair can be split across the
> mixer or across stem outputs. Pairing is refused while the other deck is
> audible, and is released automatically when you enable passthrough or vinyl

Controller mappings need no change, and a mapping that mirrors roll gestures to
the partner is fighting R8. The bench protocol repeats this in its section C.

**Perf item §9.3 — measured, no change.** The audit's verdict is that the dual
reader costs exactly what R11 says it should and nothing structural: the chunk
cache is per-`CachingReader` (80 × 8192 frames, allocated per deck whether or
not it is paired), read hints are deduplicated, the worker scheduler wakes at
most once per callback, a cache miss yields silence with the position kept, and
there are **no mutexes in the real-time read path or in `src/sources/`** at all.
A pair therefore costs 2× decode CPU and 2× cache occupancy — the intended price
of two independent data planes — and there is no shared bottleneck to remove.
Nothing was changed. The bench does not need to measure this blind: the existing
counters report it, `cachingreader.cpp:472` (cache hits/misses) and
`readaheadmanager.cpp:163` (overrun/seek accounting), which is what bench G1–G3
should be read against.

**Sanitizers.** Not run: an ASan/UBSan rebuild of `mixxx-test` is a full
recompile and was left for the developer, and `AGENTS.md` puts the sign-off in

alongside the suite run so the sanitizer evidence lands next to the bench

### 9.19 Bench round 7 (SC Live 4) — the loop encoder on the follower: deferred value carriers

**Symptom (the DJ's own sequence, no slip, no unpair).** Decks paired and
looping; the DJ turned the loop **encoder dial** on the follower to shorten the
loop, reversed it to lengthen. The loop lengths did not track across the decks,
and on ending loop mode the pair was out of sync — the two members exited with
mismatched loop states.

**Root cause.** The SC Live 4 encoder writes the raw `beatloop_size` on the
deck under the finger. Per 9.12 a the size keys were excluded from the relay
entirely (the inline echo of a size-carrying command overtook the command's own
bridge slot and applied it twice — loop_halve halved 4 → 1, T19), so the raw
write resized only the follower's replica; the edge-triggered mirror never
healed the divergence because both members' `beatloop_size` COs held *different
equal-valued states the mirror had no reason to re-copy*. The pair then ran two
different live loops until someone exited loop mode.

**Fix: deferred value carriers (`BridgeEntry::deferred`, 9.19).**
`beatloop_size` and `beatjump_size` rejoin the relay list, but their relay slot
never writes the leader from the callback. Instead it stores the **scheduled
value** plus the **leader's value at scheduling** (the baseline) into per-entry
`QAtomicInteger<qint64>` bit fields (QAtomicInteger has no FP specialization;
`qToDouble`/`qFromDouble` are gone in Qt6, so local memcpy bit casts), sets a
per-pair `deferredRelayPending` flag and schedules exactly one
`QTimer::singleShot(0)` delivery on the manager's event loop — a burst of
encoder ticks coalesces to its last value.

`deliverDeferredRelays()` clears the pending flag **first** (a write arriving
mid-delivery re-schedules rather than being lost), then re-runs the staleness
and slip gates (bridge generation, `active`, follower slip, quit-and-adopt —
R8: a roll pad pressed between schedule and delivery makes the follower's size
roll state, not a pair command). Per deferred entry:

- **quiescence by equality** — if the leader already holds the scheduled value,
  a relayed command (halve/double/sized activate) produced it through the
  leader's own engine during the event-loop turn; the echo is dropped, so 9.12
  a holds *by ordering* rather than by not relaying;
- **supersession** — if the leader holds something other than the baseline it
  had at scheduling, a newer intent owns the size; the stale carrier is dropped
  and the leader's result mirrors back;
- otherwise the value crosses under the `BridgeWriteGuard`.

Why the carrier stores the value instead of reading the follower live at
delivery: the follower's engine **re-confirms the size from its live loop grid**
(setAndConfirm) before the carrier fires, so a live read loses the encoder write
entirely — that variant shipped first and the bench showed the pair never
adopting the encoder value.

**Rejected: generation-based staleness.** A `deferredRelayGeneration` bumped by
every re-anchor was tried and removed: engaging a loop on a playing quantized
follower uses slip **internally**, and the slip-release re-anchor fired between
the encoder write and the delivery, dropping legitimate carriers. No generation
is needed: re-anchors only write follower ← leader, never the leader, so the
baseline check alone rejects exactly the superseded carriers.

**Tests.** T45 (`LoopEncoderOnFollowerResizesThePairLoop`) walks the DJ's whole
sequence — engage 4 from the follower, encoder to 2 (both members' regions
halve and stay identical), encoder back to 4, `loop_halve` applies exactly
once with the carrier in play, and reloop exit finds both members loop-off and
frame-locked. T45b covers `beatjump_size` the same way. T19 (the 9.12 a
regression) stays green.

**Harness note.** The round also repaired the shared fixture
(`deckpairtestbase.h`): the RubberBand pool is now scoped to the fixture (env
drift made its singleton assert abort any test that shifted playback), the beat
grid is attached after all four tracks load, and the stereo reference wav
(`sine-30-stereo.wav`) the fixture loads is tracked. Remaining
`ctest -R DeckPair` INTERRUPTs are pre-existing debug-build/environment issues
(double-registration and quantize asserts on non-fixture paths, the RubberBand
singleton outside the fixture) — verified failing identically at the branch
head without these changes; not introduced by 9.19.
