## 1. Summary

A **deck pair** lets two decks (1+3, or 2+4) act as one deck with one transport while keeping
two completely independent audio **data planes** (each deck keeps its own `EngineBuffer`,
reader, scalers, stem decoding, and mixer channel). The deck that receives the `pair_enabled`
write is the **pair leader**: its track is the pair's track and its transport computation is
the pair's transport. The partner is the **pair follower**.

The shared thing is the **control plane**:

- **Transport authority** — a tiny lock-free object, `EnginePairTransport`, is the single
  instance of transport truth for the pair. Each engine callback the leader publishes a
  snapshot of its pair-plane progression (`posStart/posEnd/rate/speed/playing/scratching/
  reverse`); a *locked* follower consumes the snapshot and drives its own data plane from it
  instead of computing its own transport.
- **Command effect** — transport-affecting control writes on the follower are relayed (via a
  main-thread CO bridge) to the leader's existing engine machinery, and leader status COs are
  mirrored to the follower, so *existing* control interfaces (`play`, `hotcue_*`, `loop_*`,
  `beatjump*`, `rate`, `scratch2`, `jog`, …) keep working unchanged on both decks. The only
  new CO is `pair_enabled`.
- **Slip is the per-deck escape hatch — absolutely.** `slip_enabled`, `beatlooproll_*`, and
  `reverseroll` are **never bridged in either direction**: a roll *is* slip internally
  (`loopingcontrol.cpp:1343/1783`, `ratecontrol.cpp:308–316`), so anything that engages slip
  is deck-specific by construction. On the follower, Mixxx's existing slip plane
  (`m_slipPos`) is fed from the pair transport instead of advancing locally, so "own transport
  while slipping, snap back on release" falls out of the existing `processSlip()` code path
  with no new snap logic.

Terminology note: Mixxx already uses "leader/follower" for **BPM sync** (`SyncMode`,
`EngineSync`). This feature's roles are **pair leader/pair follower**; code uses the
`DeckPair*`/`PairRole` vocabulary to keep the concepts distinct. The mechanisms are
independent (a pair leader may be a sync follower; a pair follower's sync is neutralized
while paired).

Why leader/follower rather than symmetric "pair": the design is structurally asymmetric — one
engine computes transport per callback and the other replays it. Symmetric replication of two
independent transports was evaluated and rejected (§6.1) because it cannot guarantee the
frame-exact lock the vocal/instrumental use case demands.

### Primary DJ use case (drives the acceptance tests)

The instrumental plays on deck 1 and the a cappella on deck 3 (same track on both): pair from
one deck, **mute the vocal stem on one member and solo the vocal stem on the other** (stem
mute/solo are `[ChannelN_StemM]` groups and per-deck quick-effect properties — never shared).
Both channels then play the identical sample stream from their own readers, locked
sample-for-sample through hotcues, loops, seeking, jog/scratch, wheel and pitch. Slip on the
vocal deck lets the DJ scratch or roll the a cappella independently — the instrumental and the
pair transport keep playing — and disengaging slip snaps the vocal back onto it.

## 3. Codebase facts this design leans on (all verified in this tree)

1. **One engine callback thread processes all channels sequentially** —
   `EngineMixer::processChannels` (`enginemixer.cpp:234–360`) builds `m_activeChannels` and
   processes them in a `for` loop on the "Engine" thread; `EngineWorker`s exist for effects
   only. Existing **leader-first ordering precedent**: the BPM-sync leader channel is hoisted
   to index 0 (`enginemixer.cpp:246, 303–308`). §5.6 adds pair-leader-before-follower the same
   way. Without it the follower replays the *previous* callback's transport → a constant
   one-buffer (~5–23 ms) phase offset between vocal and instrumental — audible against a stem
   pair. With it, the lock is same-callback exact.
2. **CO notifications are synchronous on the writer thread;
  no engine - thread event dispatch exists in 2.7 * *(no `ControlEventQueue`/`broadcast()`/`processControlEventSignals()`).
   `ControlDoublePrivate::setInner` emits `valueChanged` inline(`control.cpp : 287–298`);
  `ControlObject` re - emits unless the setter is itself(self - echo suppression,
   `controlobject.cpp : 48–54`).Every engine CO connection is `Qt::DirectConnection` (`enginebuffer.cpp : 130, 136,…`)because the audio thread has no event loop.Consequences : engine slots are thin(atomics / COs only, mutation deferred to `process()`);
  a main - thread bridge writing COs reaches engine slots inline with** zero added latency**;
   `QueuedConnection` bridge proxies fed from the engine thread deliver on the main loop (fine
   for status mirrors; transport never waits on the bridge).
3. **`play` is the only confirm-required deck CO** (`enginebuffer.cpp:128–133`); the engine
   validates and `setAndConfirm`s (`slotControlPlayRequest`). Mirror writes to the follower's
   `play` flow through the follower's own validation — the gate confirms but skips transport
   side effects (§5.4.5).
4. **Transport truth is in EngineBuffer.** `processTrackLocked` order
   (`enginebuffer.cpp:901–1214`): cached geometry (`track_samplerate`, `track_samples`) →
   `processSyncRequests` → `play` flag + pitch/tempo ratio → `processSlip` → `processSeek`
   (the single quantization/phase-resolution funnel, `:1366–1441`) →
   `RateControl::calculateSpeed` → keylock/scaler policy (`:954–1014`) → scaler + advance →
   **`m_playPos` = `ReadAheadManager::getFilePlaypositionFromLog()`** (`:1141–1148`) →
   controls' `setFrameInfo`+`process` → `hintReader`; then `postProcess` (after ALL decks)
   publishes `playposition` (15 Hz), `VisualPlayPosition`, `beat_distance`. **Buffer-end
   position is not integrable from rate** — loop/jump triggers, trigger-overshoot correction
   and cache-miss padding relocate the read position mid-buffer
   (`readaheadmanager.cpp:58–64, 91–97, 141–152, 169–196`) — hence the shared snapshot
   publishes `(posStart, posEnd)` pairs, never a rate-only contract. All control-class seeks
   funnel through `EngineControl::seekAbs/seekExact` (`enginecontrol.cpp:85–95`) → `EngineBuffer`.
5. **Slip = "own transport until released", already implemented.** `slip_enabled` has **no
   slot** — polled once per callback at the top of `processSlip` (`enginebuffer.cpp:1284–1337`
   `+` edge detection vs `m_bSlipEnabledProcessing`), *before* `processSeek`/`calculateSpeed`
   in the same callback. Rising edge: `m_slipPos = m_playPos`; `m_dSlipRate = m_rate_old`
   **frozen** (later pitch/scratch/sync never touch it). Advance: at the frozen rate,
   loop/repeat-aware wrap via `adjustedPositionForCurrentLoopOrRepeat`
   (`loopingcontrol.cpp:1943–1968`), `SlipModeState::Armed/Running`. Falling edge:
   `seekExact(m_slipPos)` (applied same-callback by `processSeek`) unless
   `slipQuitAndAdopt()` (`:896–899`, single caller `LoopingControl::slotBeatLoopToggle`,
   `loopingcontrol.cpp:1759` — "adopt the rolled position"). `CueControl` has zero slip
   coupling. No `slip_started`/`slip_updated` COs exist; slip state is observable only via
   `slip_enabled` + `VisualPlayPosition`. Track load fully resets slip
   (`enginebuffer.cpp:584–589`).
6. **Rate/scratch funnel**: everything speed-related passes `RateControl::calculateSpeed`
   (`ratecontrol.cpp:400–516`) — `rateSearch` override, vinyl override, scratch2 via
   `PositionScratchController` (consumes `scratch2` accumulator; PD controller + spinback;
   loop-wrap compensation via `notifyWrapAround` from the readahead seam), `jog` (read **and
   zeroed**, `:376–394`, per-RateControl low-pass), `wheel` (ControlTTRotary), temp/perm
   buttons (`processTempRate` ramps — nudge equivalent), sync-PID (`calcSyncedRate`) last,
   reverse sign last. Skipping this one function per callback neutralizes a locked follower's
   transport inputs completely — and it *must* run exactly once per pair (it mutates state).
   Two-position scratch lives on `scratch_position`/`scratch_position_enable`
   (`positionscratchcontroller.cpp:86–115`). `whirl`/`nudge` do not exist in Mixxx — their
   equivalents (`wheel`/`jog`, `rate_temp_*`) are in the relay table.
7. **Loop/cue/hotcue state**: active loop lives in `LoopingControl::m_loopInfo` + `loop_*`
   COs; hotcue/cue/intro/outro **points live in the shared `Track` cue list** (persisted via
   `CueDAO`) — two decks of one `TrackPointer` already see identical points; only per-group
   *status* (`hotcue_N_status`, temp "loop" cue id `-1`) is per-deck. The follower's replica
   controls keep state coherent by reacting to mirrored CO writes.
8. **Same-track precedent**: the clone feature (`CloneFromDeck`, `m_pChannelToCloneFrom`
   `QAtomicPointer`, `SEEK_CLONE` `enginebuffer.cpp:1397–1404`, post-load rate/pitch/loop copy
   `basetrackplayer.cpp:707–730`) proves one `Track` on two decks and gives a **one-shot**
   load-time position copy — the pattern reused at pair formation (continuous coupling is the
   snapshot, §5.2, explicitly *not* repeated clone seeks).
9. **Main-thread load funnel**: `BaseTrackPlayerImpl::slotLoadTrack` (`final`) emits
   `loadingTrack(new, old)` (`basetrackplayer.cpp:571–574`) on **every** load attempt incl.
   clears; `playerEmpty` on eject completion (`:640`); `newTrackLoaded` on success (`:747`).
   Signal-level interception covers all entry points (library, QML, AutoDJ, next-available
   deck) where `PlayerManager::slotLoadTrackToPlayer` alone does not.
10. **Load/eject stall protocol**: `m_pause` is `tryLock`ed by `process()`
    (`enginebuffer.cpp:1244`); a deck whose load/eject is in flight emits silence, does **not**
    advance `m_playPos`, and `slotTrackLoaded` **drops a pending seek** (`:593`) and resets
    slip (`:584–589`). The lock must therefore re-anchor continuously (it does — every
    callback) and after load completion (§5.7, risk 9.2).
11. **Tests are single-threaded gtest**: fixtures never start the engine thread;
    `ProcessBuffer()` = `EngineMixer::process(1024)` synchronously; `MockScaler`
    (`MockedEngineBackendTest`) makes position math exact → frame-exact assertions are
    meaningful; `BaseSignalPathTest` builds 3 decks + `addDeck()` helper + real `sine-30.wav`
    (`SignalPathTest`) with golden-buffer audio asserts; CO pulse (`set(1); set(0);`) +
    `ProcessBuffer()` is the house idiom; `MixxxTest` deletes all COs after each test.

```text
 │      │   ├─ L→F status mirror (proxies)    │   │                                    │ snapshot   │
 │      │   └─ load/eject coordination        │   │  Follower Deck3: EngineBuffer       ▼ consume   │
 │      └─ (registers decks, roles)           │   │    (own reader/scaler/stems; gates: │ slip      │

 └────────────────────────────────────────────┘   └───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- `DeckPairManager` (new, `src/mixer/deckpairmanager.{h,cpp}`) — main-thread owner of pair
  lifecycle, validation, CO bridge, load/eject coordination.
- `EnginePairTransport` (new, `src/engine/deckpairtransport.{h,cpp}`) — the shared transport
  control plane: lock-free per-callback snapshot + role/active atomics. Two instances exist
  per session (one per pair slot); **never destroyed** (pointer handoff race-free by
  construction; only flags/contents change).

## 5. Design detail

### 5.1 The new CO: `[ChannelN], pair_enabled`

- Created by `DeckPairManager` for each deck group `[Channel1]`…`[Channel4]` as the decks are
  registered (decks appear via `PlayerManager::addDeckInner`, `playermanager.cpp:331–401`; add
  one call `m_pDeckPairManager->addDeck(i, pDeck)` there). The manager is constructed in the
  `PlayerManager` ctor next to `SamplerBank` (`playermanager.cpp:136`): constructed before any
  deck, and — being an earlier child — destroyed before them; its destructor must disarm all
  pairs first, so the engine pointers it handed to buffers are consult-only, never dangling.
- Write-validated `ControlObject`: manager connects `connectValueChangeRequest`
  (DirectConnection). Every write is arbitrated:
  - **1 (enable)** on X: refuse (leave 0) unless: X unpaired; partner exists
    (`numberOfDecks()` ≥ partner index); partner is not playing (R5). Accept →
    `setAndConfirm(1)`, form pair (**X = leader**), set partner's CO to 1 (guarded write),
    start engine roles + bridge, coordinate follower's track (§5.7).
  - **0 (disable)** on either member: `setAndConfirm(0)`, tear down (order below).
  - Redundant writes are no-ops.
- Not persisted (`bPersist=false`), no alias (no `[DeckN]` groups in this tree).
- **Teardown order** (unpair): 1) stop relay/mirrors; 2) clear pair-active atomic (engine stops
  publishing/consuming next callback); 3) release the follower slip-plane feed (a mid-slip
  follower becomes a normal slipping deck, snap target frozen at the last pair plane —
  documented, T31); 4) restore follower sync/vinyl acceptability. No position writes needed:
  the follower's `m_playPos` already *is* the pair plane.

### 5.2 `EnginePairTransport` — the shared transport control plane

```cpp
// src/engine/deckpairtransport.h
enum class PairRole : quint8 { Unpaired = 0, PairLeader = 1, PairFollower = 2 };

// Lock-free single-producer (leader buffer, engine thread) / single-consumer (follower
// buffer, engine thread, same callback, ordered after the leader). Publish/consume inside
// each buffer's m_pause window (same discipline as m_queuedSeek / ControlValueAtomic).
struct PairTransportSnapshot {
    double posStart, posEnd;   // pair-plane frame positions at leader buffer start/end
                               // (loop/trigger-wrap/seek aware — NOT rate-integrable)
    double rate, speed;        // applied sample-consumption rate; track-time/real-time speed
    bool playing, scratching, reverse;  // drive follower paused-flag + scaler policy (§5.4.1)
    quint32 seq;               // seqlock: odd = write in progress
};

class EnginePairTransport {             // one per pair slot, static lifetime
  public:
    void publish(const PairTransportSnapshot&);     // leader, engine thread
    bool consume(PairTransportSnapshot* out) const; // follower, engine thread
    QAtomicInteger<quint8> roleLeader, roleFollower; // set/cleared by DeckPairManager (main)
    QAtomicInteger<quint8> active;                  // pair live & transport shared
};
```

- **Pair plane** (the transport members lock to): leader slipping → the leader's slip plane
  `m_slipPos` (which keeps advancing during slip, with loop wraps); otherwise the leader's
  `m_playPos`. One expression, both cases. Consequences, all from existing slip semantics:
  leader slips → follower keeps playing the uninterrupted pair plane; leader slip-end rejoins
  it; a seek made on the leader during its slip moves the leader's *audible* plane only.
- Delivery into buffers: `EngineBuffer::setPairTransport(EnginePairTransport*, PairRole)`
  storing `QAtomicPointer<EnginePairTransport> m_pPairTransport` + `QAtomicInteger<quint8>
  m_pairRole` (handoff pattern precedented by clone's `m_pChannelToCloneFrom` `QAtomicPointer`,
  `enginebuffer.h:492`); surfaced through `EngineDeck` (`getEngineBuffer()` passthrough).
  Objects are never freed → no lifetime race; behavior gated by `active` + `role`.
- `isPairLockedFollower()` (engine-thread helper on `EngineBuffer`): `role == PairFollower &&
  active && m_slipModeState == Disabled && !slipEnabledNow` — reads the same
  `m_bSlipEnabledProcessing` the process path uses. **Locked mode** = consume the snapshot and
  suppress local transport effect.

### 5.3 Leader-side EngineBuffer changes (minimal diff, in `processTrackLocked`)

1. After the position-advance step, if `role == PairLeader && active`: publish
   `{posStart, posEnd, rate, speed, playing, scratching, reverse}` — the values the buffer
   just used/produced for its **main plane** (slip-plane values when the leader itself is
   slipping, per §5.2).
2. No other leader behavior changes. The leader's loops, cues, quantize, sync, vinyl, repeat
   remain its own engine machinery, unchanged. The pair transport *is* a fully-featured
   single-deck transport; only one copy of it runs.

### 5.4 Follower-side changes (the gates)

All gates read the §5.2 predicate once per callback (slot-side gates per invocation — they run
on the writer thread and touch atomics/COs only, the existing slot discipline):

1. **Position consumption** — in `processTrackLocked`, when locked: after `processSeek` and
   before scaling, `m_playPos := snapshot.posStart`; use snapshot `rate/speed` for scaler
   configuration instead of `RateControl::calculateSpeed` output (skipping the call alone
   neutralizes rate, temp/perm, wheel, **jog/scratch2 consumption**, reverse, sync-PID —
   fact §3.6), and adopt snapshot `scratching/reverse` for the keylock/scaler policy so both
   members choose the same scaler mode; after the scaler step set `m_playPos :=
   snapshot.posEnd` (authoritative overwrite over the read-log result → **drift structurally
   impossible**; frame-exact in MockScaler tests). `m_speed_old/m_rate_old/m_actual_speed`
   bookkeeping follows the snapshot so scaler re-selection behaves. The follower keeps its own
   `ReadAheadManager`/`CachingReader`/scalers, fed from the shared positions (data plane
   independent per R11).
2. **Seek suppression** — `queueNewPlaypos`/`seekAbs`/`seekExact` early-return when locked
   (before any crossfade-rescaler side effects — a re-seek mid-lock would click). All
   quantization/phase resolution for the pair happens once, on the leader's `processSeek`
   (fact §3.4); the follower's replica controls still react to mirrored COs (loop points,
   hotcue statuses) but their seeks are no-ops. The follower replica is display/slip-ready
   state, self-healing via L→F mirrors.
3. **Slip plane feed (the only slip interaction in the design)** — in `processSlip`, when
   `role == PairFollower && active && slipping`: instead of advancing `m_slipPos` at the
   frozen `m_dSlipRate` with local loop simulation, set `m_slipPos :=` leader pair plane
   (already loop- and seek-aware by construction). `m_slipModeState` is still set from the
   follower's own loop replica so waveform slip indicators stay coherent. Slip enable
   (rising-edge snapshot `m_slipPos = m_playPos` — already the pair plane while locked) and
   disable (`seekExact(m_slipPos)` → pair plane, same callback) need **no new code**: that is
   the snap-back. While slipping the follower is unlocked → its transport slots/seek/rate work
   locally with zero special cases (scratch, roll, hotcue-goto behave as on any slipping deck;
   the frozen slip rate guarantees scratch never perturbs the snap target).
4. **Publish nothing** — only the leader publishes; the follower can never move the pair plane
   twice.
5. **`slotControlPlayRequest` and friends** — when locked, still validate/confirm (so the
   mirrored `play` value lands) but skip transport effects and phase-sync requests
   (`requestSyncPhase`); the leader owns those. EOT is evaluated identically on both members
   (same pair position); the leader's `play=0` release at end-of-track is mirrored —
   idempotent (T21).
6. **`processSyncRequests`, BpmControl/KeyControl/ClockControl/QuantizeControl,
   `updateIndicators`, `hintReader`, `postProcess*`, `VisualPlayPosition`** — no changes:
   consistent automatically because the follower's position *is* the pair plane and its rate
   COs are mirrored leader values (`local_bpm`, `beat_distance`, `key`, `pitch`, `beat_active`
   recompute correctly per-deck — no mirror needed, keeping the mirror table small).
7. **Load interlock**: `slotTrackLoaded` resets slip and drops pending seeks (fact §3.10) —
   correct for pair loads; the lock re-anchors from the next consumed callback. `m_channelCount`
   may differ transiently while the follower loads — snapshot consumption guards on
   `isTrackLoaded()` and the follower emits silence (with play still mirrored) until loaded.

### 5.5 The CO bridge: relay (F→L) and mirror (L→F)

Owned by `DeckPair` (main thread), implemented with `ControlProxy`s parented to it (create/
destroy on main thread — required). Two static key tables + echo quiescence (needed because
trigger buttons use `ignoreNops=false` and always re-emit; value COs quiesce naturally via
`pSender` self-echo suppression + `ignoreNops=true` default — fact §3.2). Phase 2 implements
the quiescence as *equality*: a bridge write is skipped when its target already carries the
value — see deviation note 9.11 b).

- **Relay F→L** (only while bridged **and the follower is not slipping**; runs inline on the
  writing main thread ⇒ zero added latency to transport):
  `play, start_play, start_stop, stop, start, end, playposition, repeat, quantize, cue_*`
  (set/clear/goto/gotoandplay/gotoandstop/cue_default/cue_cdj/cue_play/play_stutter/
  cue_preview incl. press+release pairs), `hotcue_N_{
    activate, set, setcue, setloop, goto,
            gotoandplay, gotoandstop, gotoandloop, cueloop, activate*, clear, swap, color
}`,
  `loop_in, loop_out, loop_exit, loop_enabled, reloop_toggle, reloop_andstop, loop_remove,
  loop_anchor, loop_move*, loop_scale, loop_halve, loop_double, beatloop*, beatloop_size,
  beatjump*, beatjump_size*, rate, rate_range, rate_dir, rate_temp_*, rate_perm_*, reverse,
  fwd, back, wheel, scratch2_enable, scratch_position, scratch_position_enable,
  pitch_adjust, keylock, keylockMode, keyunlockMode, bpm (user-set path),
  beats_adjust_*/translate_*, bpm_tap, tempo_tap, sort_hotcues*, intro/outro set/clear`
  (accumulator steering for `jog`/`scratch2`, below).
  - **Accumulator steering** (`jog`, `scratch2` — consumed-and-zeroed by their owner,
    fact §3.6): relay slot does `L.co += F.co->get(); F.co->set(0)` (race-free: writers and
    relay are main-thread synchronous; the follower's consumer is skipped while locked).
    `scratch_position` relays as a plain value (not an accumulator).
  - The follower's own engine objects *also* react to their group's CO writes — intended:
    it maintains a live replica (loop points, hotcue statuses, rate state) that slip-engage
    can take over instantly and that skins/controllers display (§5.4.2).
- **Mirror L→F** (status/values, guarded): `play` (lands via F's confirm path, §5.4.5),
  `play_indicator, cue_indicator, play_latched, loop_enabled, loop_start_position,
  loop_end_position, beatloop_size, beatloop_%s_enabled, loop_anchor, cue_point,
  hotcue_N_status, hotcue_N_type, hotcue_N_position, hotcue_N_endposition, hotcue_N_color,
  intro/outro position+enabled+status, quantize, repeat, rate, rate_ratio, reverse, keylock,
  pitch_adjust, pitch, keylockMode, keyunlockMode, beatloop legacy mirror`.
  Deliberately **not mirrored** (recomputed coherently per §5.4.6): `playposition,
  beat_distance, local_bpm, bpm (engine-written side), key, pitch display,
  beat_next/prev/closest, beat_active, time elapsed/remaining, VisualPlayPosition`.
  Deliberately **never bridged, in either direction** (unit-testable invariant, T30): any
  slip key — `slip_enabled, beatlooproll_%s_activate, beatlooproll_r%s_activate,
  reverseroll` — plus all `[ChannelN_StemM]` COs, all mixer-strip COs, `vinylcontrol_*`,
  `sync_*`, `eject` (§5.7), `pair_enabled` (§5.1), `passthrough` (§5.8).
- **No slip coupling anywhere.** The follower's only slip-related code is the §5.4.3
  slip-plane feed (a position source while its own slip is engaged). There is no slip-state
  mirroring, arming window, or origin tracking — the bridge is a pure two-table lookup.
- **Ordering note**: manager proxies connect default `AutoConnection` to main-thread slots →
  engine-originated writes queue onto the main loop (display-rate statuses; transport never
  waits on the bridge); main-thread user writes relay synchronously inline.
  `ControlObject::set(key,…)` static (pSender=nullptr, bypasses self-echo suppression) is
  forbidden inside the bridge; all bridge writes go through the proxies.

### 5.6 Engine callback ordering (new: `EngineMixer` touch)

`processChannels` already hoists the sync-leader channel first (`enginemixer.cpp:246, 303–308`).
Add: while building `m_activeChannels`, hoist/insert each active pair's **leader before its
follower** (≤2 pairs; O(1) post-pass using `EngineBuffer::pairInfo()` atomics — e.g. move the
follower entry to immediately after the leader's). Query surface: `EngineMixer` asks each
channel's buffer for `activePairTransport()/role` (atomics; no buffer→buffer pointer
chasing). This guarantees the follower consumes a **same-callback** snapshot (§3.1: without
it, a permanent one-buffer stem offset; regression test T10 fails loudly).

### 5.7 Load / eject coordination (deck layer)

- **Pair formation**: if follower's loaded track ≠ leader's, follower loads the leader's
  track with `play = leader.play`, **preserving the follower's own stem selection** — reuse
  the clone path but *not* `cloneStemState` (`basetrackplayer.cpp:406–413`): a
  `slotCloneFromGroup` variant with a `StemChannelSelection` override parameter (or load +
  re-apply the follower stem mask). `SEEK_CLONE` (`m_pChannelToCloneFrom`, one-shot; note it
  reads the leader's `getExactPlayPos()` → VisualPlayPosition, which is exactly what we want
  at formation) aligns the first buffer; the lock maintains it afterwards.
- **Load on either member while paired** → load the same track on both: intercept
  `BaseTrackPlayer::loadingTrack(new, old)` (fires for *every* load entry point — fact §3.9),
  guarded against A→B→A bounce. Request on leader → follower `slotLoadTrack(new, bPlay=false)`
  (pair position authority continues; if leader is playing, follower loads with
  `bPlay=leader.play`). Request on follower → treated as a pair load: apply to the leader too
  (`pLeader->slotLoadTrack(new, …)`); the leader's transport restarts on the new track per
  normal load semantics.
- **Eject on either** → eject both: watch `playerEmpty`/`trackUnloaded`; guarded eject on the
  partner. Pair stays engaged (§2 R7 corollary).
- **Invariant**: `DEBUG_ASSERT` in the bridge (cheap `PlayerInfo` compare) that paired members
  hold the same track id (a divergence would mean a missed funnel).
- AutoDJ note: an AutoDJ handoff onto a paired deck loads both decks — correct and desirable;
  AutoDJ's own deck-selection logic is untouched.

### 5.8 Neutralizations & known shared-metadata effects

- **Sync on the follower**: `connectValueChangeRequest` bounce — refuse
  `sync_enabled/sync_mode/sync_leader` non-zero writes on the follower while locked (snap back
  to 0 + `qWarning`). Justification: a follower-side sync phase/tempo PID would
  double-correct against the pair lock (`calcSyncedRate`, `updateLeaderBpm` writing
  `rate_ratio`, `synccontrol.cpp:254–273`; `beatsync` phase seek), and an active follower
  Syncable would negotiate leadership for what is one transport (EngineSync leadership churn).
  Debug-assert `follower.syncMode == None` in the lock. Precedent: `SyncControl` already
  excludes follower+vinyl (`synccontrol.cpp:155–160`).
- **Vinyl control on the follower while locked**: refuse
  `vinylcontrol_wantenabled/vinylcontrol_enabled` (same bounce mechanism). Vinyl on the leader
  works normally (it *is* transport).
- **Passthrough / live input on either member**: refuse pairing while on; if toggled on a
  member mid-pair, disband the pair (`EngineDeck::process` short-circuits *before*
  `EngineBuffer::process`, `enginedeck.cpp:232–243` — the transport and the lock would
  freeze).
- **Shared `Track` cue list** (both decks share cue objects — fact §3.7): hotcue/intro/outro
  point edits on either deck affect both — consistent with "one deck". Known edges, accepted
  for v1 and documented: edits made while a member is slipping mutate the shared cues
  (last-writer-wins); the **temp loop cue (hotcue id −1)** is written by each deck's
  `LoopingControl::updateLoopCue` (`loopingcontrol.cpp:1912–1940`) — while locked the
  follower's loop points are mirrors → same-value writes; suppress-if-proven-needed hook
  reserved for Phase 5. Beatgrid edits (`beats_adjust_*`, `bpm_tap`) are shared-track metadata
  by nature — relayed once to the leader so a single write path applies them.
- **Slip-time hotcue SET on a slipping follower** stores the point at the follower's *audible*
  position into the shared Track (`frameInfo().currentPosition` semantics, existing) —
  documented edge, consistent with shared cue lists.

## 6. Alternatives considered (and why the chosen design is simpler/safer)

### 6.1 Bidirectional CO replication with two live transports (rejected)

Mirror every transport CO both ways and let both decks' full engine stacks run "as if".
Rejected: two state machines fed by eventually-equal command streams are **not** frame-exact
(quantize/loop decisions sample the position at write time; buffer-end position is not
integrable, fact §3.4) — for vocal+instrumental any drift is heard as flam/flange. Also
doubles every transport side effect (seek crossfades, sync PID, indicator state machines,
`calculateSpeed` mutation).

### 6.2 Share the data plane (one buffer feeding two channels) (rejected)

Explicitly out per requirements (R11); breaks per-deck stem gains/mutes and FX, which live
inside the data plane; makes unpairing a discontinuity.

### 6.3 One shared set of engine control objects owned by the leader; follower's destroyed

Cleanest "single brain", but destroys the follower's live replica (needed at slip-engage),
breaks the "every deck answers its own group's COs" invariant (skins/controllers read
`[Channel3]` COs nothing owns anymore), and makes unpair large engine-graph surgery. The
chosen design keeps both object graphs intact and symmetric; only gate flags + a snapshot.

### 6.4 Two EngineBuffers sharing one control group

Rejected (would fight over `play` confirm-required handlers, `playposition`
seek ping-pong, `jog`/`scratch2` consumption stealing, the per-group `VisualPlayPosition`
singleton — research items D–H). Each member keeps its own full group; the pair adds only
the out-of-band transport snapshot.

### 6.5 Symmetric "pair" terminology

The transport authority is structurally asymmetric (§1) — leader/follower names document the
data flow honestly. (Sync's leader/follower is BPM-only; new types are `DeckPair*`-prefixed.)

## 9. Risks, open items, mitigations

1. **Same-callback ordering coupling** (§5.6): a future channel-parallelizing refactor must
   re-establish leader-before-follower. T10 fails loudly on regression (one-buffer offset);
   `EnginePairTransport` could later gain staleness tolerance without interface change.
2. **Load/eject stall races** (fact §3.10): loading deck freezes position, drops pending
   seeks, resets slip. Mitigated by continuous re-anchoring (lock), one-shot `SEEK_CLONE` at
   formation, and explicit test T39.
3. **Dual `CachingReader`s of one Track**: 2× chunk-cache memory + 2× `hintAndMaybeWake`
   wakes on the shared `EngineWorkerScheduler`; a follower cache miss writes **silence but
   keeps position** (audio glitch, no desync). Phase-5 perf item (prefetch follows the pair
   plane because `hintReader` runs on consumed positions).
4. **`DirectConnection` discipline**: bridge slots write only COs/atomics and are
   reentrancy safe (T37). The relay refuses exactly one origin: writes issued from inside
   the audio callback on the callback thread (engine-internal status), via
   `EngineMixer::isEngineCallbackThread()`; commands from every other thread — GUI *and
   the controller engine thread, which is how hardware delivers input* — relay inline,
   exactly like a direct controller write to an unpaired deck (supersedes the original
   "main-thread only" rule; see 9.11 h).
5. **Replica divergence** (follower loop/cue replica vs leader): self-healing mirror
   overwrites; worst case is a sub-buffer decision diff that never reaches audio while locked
   and is corrected before slip-engage exposes it.
6. **Shared-track metadata** (§5.8): slip-time cue edits, temp-loop-cue contention — accepted
   and documented; suppress-if-proven-needed hook in Phase 5.
7. **Tunable behaviors flagged for bench testing** (each is a table entry, not architecture):
   auto-disband vs persist on eject; `keylock`/`pitch_adjust` shared vs per-deck; follower
   rate-slider mirror granularity (potmeter flood rate); **all-or-nothing pair load/eject**
   instead of leaving a `Reject`-blocked pair momentarily divergent (§2 corollaries; needs
   the Phase-3 play-state mirror to be expressible, so it is a Phase-5 bench item).
   *(Roll-coupling tunable removed with the v2 re-spec.)*
8. **EOT/`end_of_track`**: `end_of_track` CO is vestigial in 2.7 (nothing sets it to 1);
   EOT play-release is mirrored and idempotent (T21).
9. **Interaction with deck clone & `[Controls]AllowTrackLoadToPlayingDeck`**: clone onto a
   paired deck loads both; double-tap-clone (`playermanager.cpp:628–653`) relies on the
   bridge's quiescence-by-equality (a clone-load writes values the target already carries
   for a pair, so no recursive mirroring) — T6.
10. **CPU/memory**: two static `EnginePairTransport` instances; proxies ≈ bridge-table size
    per pair (mirror list kept minimal via per-deck recomputation §5.4.6); zero per-callback
    allocations (POD snapshot).

### 9.11 Phase 2 implementation notes (transport lock, landed; deviations from the plan)

Implemented in `EngineBuffer` (publish/consume + gates), `EngineMixer` (§5.6 ordering),
`RateControl` (locked-follower slot gates) and the `DeckPairManager` CO bridge (Phase-2
subset of the §5.5 tables). Exit tests T9–T12, T14–T15, T21, T24, T39 green
(`src/test/deckpair_test.cpp`; T12/T21 need real scalers — the mock scaler bypasses the
reader, so loop wrap/repeat never happen on it — and run in a `BaseSignalPathTest` fixture).

Deviations found while implementing:

 a) **`playposition` mirror (supersedes §5.4.6/§5.5 "not mirrored" for locked pairs):**
    a locked follower stops writing its own playposition (its `updateIndicators` would race
    the bridge over the same CO), and the leader's playposition is mirrored L→F instead.
 b) **Quiescence by equality replaces the planned global `m_bridging` flag:** the flag also
    swallowed *legitimate* derived mirrors inside a relay cascade (e.g. `rate_ratio`, derived
    by the leader's RateControl from a slider move relayed from the follower). Bridge slots
    now drop a write whose target already carries the value — every bridged write makes
    target == source, so echoes (including `ignoreNops=false` button re-emits, T37) terminate
    at the first repetition; no recursion beyond one echo round is reachable. Equality is
    necessary but not sufficient for continuously-queued mirrors — see the echo guard (l).
 c) **Owner-only (read-only) status COs** — `hotcue_N_status/_type`, `beatloop_*_enabled`,
    `play_latched` — refuse proxy `set()` (their owners write them with `forceSet`); the

 l) **Bench finding #1 — pitch-fader limit cycle → bridge echo guard (`BridgeWriteGuard`):**
    the first real-rig test (2026-10-21) heard a ±2% engine-level pitch oscillation whenever
    a pitch fader was moved on *either* member, superposing to a second ±2% step and an
    audible glitch when the other member's fader moved too. Mechanism: slider↔ratio are each
    bridged in both directions, relays run inline on the writing (often controller) thread,
    while mirrors are *queued* to the main loop — a mirror that lands late carries a stale
    leader value, and its echo into the relay looked like a fresh command, rolling the leader
    back underneath the user's newest fader position: a stale-ghost feedback loop between the
    two members. Fix, two layers:
      - *Echo guard* (`BridgeWriteGuard`, per-thread depth): while the bridge itself is
        writing, the relay refuses to propagate — the echo of a bridge write is delivery,
        never a command.
      - *Mirror freshness ordering*: a mirror emission that lands with a value the leader no
        longer holds is a stale ghost and is dropped (the freshest queued emission carries
        the current value); applying it would clobber the follower's newer state, and since
        its echo is deliberately not relayed, nothing would heal it.
    Equality quiescence (b) stays as the same-value backstop. Regression test
    `DeckPairEngineTest.StaleQueuedMirrorIsNotRelayedBack` pins the observable property
    (leader keeps its newest value, both members agree after queued delivery); negative
    control: removing the freshness check fails it with exactly the clobber signature. The
    echo guard alone is not separately observable in the synchronous test env — it covers
    deep engine cascades and is defence in depth.

    buttons and BPM adjust: clean, no artefacts. The simultaneous two-fader scenario is not
    physically reproducible on the current rig (one operator, one controller); it is covered
    by the automated stale-mirror test above, and a sequential rapid fader alternation
    (leader, then follower, within the settle time; the pair must always settle at the last
    moved fader) is retained as an open bench item until two-fader hardware is available.
 m) **Bench finding #2 — roll and slicer pads countermanded → mirror halted during slip:**

    landed as a seek on the rolling deck at engine rate and dragged it back onto the pair
    plane every callback; a mirrored loop status overwrote the deck-local roll window.

    bridge symmetric with the relay side (both directions silent, hence "the bridge is
    disconnected while slipping") and supersedes note e's per-item exception for `play`.
    Replica self-healing is no longer continuous — it moves to slip release:
    `refreshMirrorValues()` (extracted from bridge formation, same one-shot bulk copy, same
    quiescence) runs once when the follower's `slip_enabled` drops to 0 while the pair is
    still active. Two details that fall out of the bulk copy being out of engine order:
      - *Mirror table order:* the loop points are listed before `loop_enabled` (they are
        adjacent in §5.5 anyway). Event-by-event delivery is already ordered by the leader's
        own engine; the bulk copy is table-ordered, and the follower's `LoopingControl`
        refuses an enable without a valid region, so a cold replica would drop the loop
        state permanently (`loopingcontrol.cpp:1020-1035`).
      - *Delivery thread:* the slip listener uses the default (auto) connection, not a
        direct one. `EngineBuffer::slotTrackLoaded` resets slip from the load worker thread
        (`enginebuffer.cpp:609-610`), so a direct listener would run the bulk CO write off the
        main thread; auto queues it onto the main loop. From the main thread — GUI, and the
        unit tests, where `ProcessBuffer()` runs the callback on the main thread — delivery
        stays synchronous, which is what the regression test asserts.
    `slotBridgeMirror` re-reads `slip_enabled` at delivery time, so a queued re-anchor whose
    follower has been rolled again in the meantime is dropped rather than clobbering the new
    slip. Regression tests: `DeckPairEngineTest.MirrorPausedWhileFollowerSlipsAndReanchors`

    follower wraps twice inside its own 2-beat window while the leader plays clear through
    it, then rejoins frame-exact on release). Negative control: with the play-only gate of
    note e, the second test fails as the pair plane reaches the roll window — the audible

### 9.12 Phase 3 implementation notes (command bridge, landed; deviations from the plan)

Implemented in the `DeckPairManager` CO bridge (full §5.5 tables + accumulator steering),
`CueControl` (locked-follower preview gate), and `EngineBuffer` (adoption quiescence flag).
Exit tests T16–T20, T22–T23, T30 (both parts), T37 green (`src/test/deckpair_test.cpp`,
`DeckPairManagerTest.BridgeTables*`), plus T16b `JogAccumulatorCrossesThePairLaneExactlyOnce`
and the reworked T16 measurement from bench rounds 2–3 (notes i / j). Written autonomously by the AI assistant as a
continuation of §9.11; the human developer must bench-verify on real hardware.

 a) **Status keys that the engine rewrites as a *side effect* of a relayed command must not
    be relayed** (supersedes §5.5 listing `loop_enabled`, `beatloop_size`, `beatjump_size`
    as F→L relays — they stay mirror-only): the follower's own engine reacts to the relayed
    command (replica, intended) and rewrites the status key; that rewrite is
    indistinguishable from a user command to the thread-based origin filter, so it would
    relay back and apply twice. Two concrete failures found by the tests: `reloop_toggle`
    exited then immediately *re-entered* the leader's loop (follower status 0 arriving
    before the relayed toggle inverts a toggle), and `loop_halve` halved the pair loop by
    4× (the follower's halve wrote `beatloop_size` 4→2, which the relayed `loop_halve` then
    halved again). Commands stay fully relayed; sizes/enable states flow leader→follower.

 b) **Mid-roll loop adoption needs its own quiescence window** (found by T30):
    `slipQuitAndAdopt()` clears the follower's `slip_enabled` *synchronously inside* the
    command handler that the follower's own engine runs before the bridge listener, so the
    "slip disconnects the bridge" guard (9.11 m) sees the slip already gone and would relay
    the adoption (`beatloop_activate` pressed while rolling) onto the leader. EngineBuffer
    now keeps the adoption pending until its callback processes the slip release
    (`isSlipQuitAndAdoptPending()`, also cleared on track load — a pending adoption against
    an unloaded track must not linger); the relay treats that window as slipping. R8's
    "mid-roll `beatloop_activate` is a local act with zero pair effect" is pinned by
    `DeckPairEngineTest.SlipStaysOnTheDeckThatEngagedIt`.

 c) **Preview machinery must not run on a locked follower** (found by T18): the follower's
    own `hotcueActivatePreview` writes `play` inline on the writer thread, *ahead of* the
    relayed button press, so the leader takes its already-playing branch (plain goto, no
    preview/return state) and the return-on-release is lost; the same happens on release in
    reverse. `CueControl` now skips preview handling while `isPairLockedFollower()` — the
    command reaches the leader via the bridge and the follower rides the pair plane; its
    preview status arrives on the mirror. (Cue-preview CO `cue_preview` gated likewise;
    `play_stutter`/`cue_default` branches were examined and left ungated — they are
    idempotent through the play mirror, T23 pins the observable behaviour.) Note this
    tree's single-deck preview-release semantics: the deck stops *at the previewed cue
    point* (the cached return position is the cue position, not the pre-preview position) —
    the pair reproduces exactly that.

 d) **Steered accumulator set is {`jog`} only, not {`jog`, `scratch2`}** (found by
    T16; final shape by note i): the plan (fact §3.6) assumed `scratch2` is
    consumed-and-zeroed by its owner; RateControl only *reads* it — the scratch source
    (mapping/vinyl) owns the value contract, and nothing zeroes it. Copy-relay (leader
    mirrors the follower's value) is therefore the correct semantic for `scratch2`;
    steering it (add + zero) would integrate absolute velocity writes into a runaway
    value. `wheel` keeps the plain value copy as well: it is a self-decaying
    `ControlTTRotary` with an absolute-value contract, which add-steering would multiply
    across successive notches; bench round 2 confirmed wheel and scratch feel identical
    on both members. Only `jog` — whose single consumer reads-and-zeroes it — needs any
    special transfer at all, and it turns out that transfer cannot live in the CO bridge
    (notes i and j): it rides the pair transport's accumulator lane.

 e) **The shared-Track beatgrid family (`bpm`, `beats_adjust_*`, `beats_translate_*`) is
    not relayed** (§5.5 listed it): the edit mutates the one Track both members share and
    the acting deck's BpmControl applies it once; both engines adopt the new grid via the
    Track's beats-changed signal, so a relay would double-apply the translation. Tempo
    *results* still move the pair through the `rate_ratio` net (9.11 d).

 f) **Naming in this tree** (extends 9.11 f): `cue_play` (no `cue_cue_play`),
    `loop_move_<size>_forward|backward` (no `_1_beat_` spelling); the Phase 2 hotcue loop
    also had an off-by-one (`hotcue_0_*` does not exist, `hotcue_8` was silently dropped) —
    fixed, the range is 1..`kMaxNumberOfHotcues`, both tables.

 g) **`pitch`/`key` are not mirrored** (§5.4.6 over §5.5's "pitch" entry): the follower
    recomputes them at the pair rate; T22 pins that leader and follower agree within a
    cent. The `pitch` CO publishes **cents** in this tree (100 = one semitone).

 h) **A locked follower still runs the skipped accumulator consumer** (bench round 1;
    cited by the `EngineBuffer`/`RateControl` code): skipping `calculateSpeed()` also skips
    `getJogFactor()`'s read-and-zero and low-pass decay. The consumer step is what *gives*
    the accumulator a meaning, and on a pair it must still happen — the follower runs it
    once per callback and its consumed value is what crosses to the leader (note j, which
    supersedes the value-copy and drain-move transfers proposed here first).

 i) **Bench findings #3/#4 (rounds 2+3, SC Live 4) — no CO-level transfer of `jog` can
    work:** round 2 heard follower nudges at 2–3x strength (leader right, scratch right);
    the fix tried there — a drain-additive move (`L += F; F = 0`) in the relay — cut it to
    a uniform 2x in round 3, leader still right, scratch still right. The bench machine's
    shared component library then supplied the missing fact: `JogWheelBasic` writes the
    deck's `jog` control with **absolute per-message velocity** (the SC Live's wheel
    reports absolute platter position; the mapping derives deltas and *sets* `jog`), while
    skins and `+=` nudge writers **accumulate deltas** into the same control. A single
    deck reconciles both contracts only because its consumer read-and-zeroes at a fixed
    period: overwrite-vs-sum is decided *at the consumption point*, and no event-time
    relay can know which contract wrote the value. The copy relay re-fed cumulative
    `+=` deltas (2–3x); the drain-move summed absolute velocity messages — ~2 per
    callback window at bench buffer sizes is exactly the uniform 2x that remained.
     Resolution in note j; both failed transfers are pinned by the round-3 test pair
     (`absolute writes must not sum`, `relative bursts must not duplicate`).

 j) **`jog` crosses on the pair transport's accumulator lane, not the CO bridge**
    (implementation of note i): the locked follower's per-callback consume step
    (`EngineBuffer` → `RateControl::pairFollowerConsumeAccumulators()`, note h) now
    *publishes* what its read-and-zero discarded onto a latest-wins slot in
    `EnginePairTransport`; the pair leader's `RateControl::getJogFactor()` **claims** the
    lane into its own consumption, exactly where a single deck would have consumed the
    same value. Both contracts then behave single-deck-exactly for free: overwrite
    happens on the follower's CO before its consume (latest velocity), accumulation
    happens there too (`+=` bursts sum), and each value reaches the leader's rate
    precisely once. One publish per follower callback makes the slot latest-wins (a
    stalled leader — loading — cannot accumulate a stale lump). Latency: one callback,
    which is the delay a post-consumption write has on a single deck anyway. `jog` is
    removed from the relay table; the table-invariant test pins its absence from both
    tables because every CO transfer of it failed the bench while passing write-once
    tests. NaNs are dropped at publish (poisoned wheel positions) as `getJogFactor`
    drops them; `reset()` silences the lane with the pair. Regression:
    `DeckPairEngineTest.JogAccumulatorCrossesThePairLaneExactlyOnce` (negative control:
    publishing a constant or a sum, or skipping the claim, fails it outright) and the
    reworked T16, which now measures the *steady-state* rate effect per buffer (the old
    window-sum thresholds were effect-zero tolerant — the very test weakness the bench
    kept discovering).

*Phase-4 carry-overs unchanged:* re-sync the follower's internal rate state before
slip-engage takes over (9.11 d), and the 9.11 m bench item (two-fader rapid alternation).
